## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
The Spanish named it "El Camino Real," meaning "The Royal Road", but for many indigenous tribes like the Caddo and Atakapan people—it was simply their home. Geologically speaking, the trail cuts across three significant eco-regions—the piney woods of East Texas and Western Louisiana; central Texas' Post Oak Savannah, known for its majestic live oaks; and the brush country of South Texas.
