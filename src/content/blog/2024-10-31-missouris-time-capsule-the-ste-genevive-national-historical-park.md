## **Introduction**

Welcome to an atmospheric journey through time nestled on the western bank of the mighty Mississippi River. Preserving one of the earliest European settlements in the region, Ste. Geneviève National Historical Park is much more than just a destination - it’s a portal into America's French colonial past.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

Founded around 1735 by French Canadian colonists, Ste. Geneviève quickly became a thriving community thanks to its lucrative lead mining industry and fertile farmlands. The park houses several well-preserved examples of its unique architectural heritage, including vertical log homes known as ‘poteaux-en-terre’ (post-in-ground). 

Steeped in culture, a visit here provides an immersible experience that brings to life tales of fur traders, farmers, enslaved Africans, and indigenous tribesmen who once called this place home.

## **Activities Guide**

The park offers myriad opportunities to dive headfirst into our nation’s rich tapestry—engage with living history performances that capture everyday life from centuries ago or embark on self-guided tours through historic homes like Beauvais-Amoureux House or Jean-Baptiste Valle House.

Don’t miss out on exploring La Maison de Guibourd, which showcases French Creole architecture at its finest while encapsulating layers upon layers of historical narratives within its walls.

Periodic events such as traditional cooking demonstrations, candle making workshops, and old-time music sessions will deepen your appreciation of the past while ensuring an enjoyable and enlightening visit.

## **Visitor Information**

The park is open year-round, with guided tours available from May through October. Visitors can experience the park independently using interpretive signage and maps during other times. While there’s no entrance fee for the park itself, some historic homes charge a nominal admission cost. The visitor center, located in the heart of Ste. Geneviève's historic district, offers insightful orientation films, exhibits, as well as book & souvenir shopping facilities.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**

Families with young children will appreciate intriguing storytelling sessions that bring history to life, while photographers would be awestruck by the beautifully preserved 18th-century structural artistry scattered across the park.

For those interested in genealogical research or historical studies; access to an extensive collection of documents related to French colonial America at the Centre de Recherches Historiques de Ste. Geneviève could be an invaluable resource.

## **Regional Context**

Just an hour south of St.Louis via I-55, Ste.Genevieve serves not only as a perfect day trip destination but also as a gateway to more regional delights including nearby Hawn State Park's pristine trails, the bustling wine trail around Route du Vin, and charismatic riverboat cruises along Mississippi River.
