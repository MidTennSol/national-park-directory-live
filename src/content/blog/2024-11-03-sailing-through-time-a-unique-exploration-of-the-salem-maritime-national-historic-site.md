## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
Established in 1938, Salem Maritime was America’s first National Historic Site, reflecting its significance in our country's narrative. The site encompasses nearly nine acres of land and water along with twelve historic structures dating back to the 17th century when Salem was among New England’s largest international ports.
 
Dotted with iconic landmarks like Derby House - America's oldest surviving brick mansion or the Friendship - a replica of an East Indiaman trading vessel – each holding stories from an era gone by - stories of explorers venturing into unknown worlds and traders changing the face of global economy.

## **Visitor Information**
The Salem Maritime National Historic Site is free to visit, excluding guided tours and special events, which carry a nominal fee. The site operates from 9 AM to 5 PM, but hours may vary per season. On-site amenities include restrooms, a bookstore, and wheelchair-accessible facilities. While pets are welcome on park grounds, they’re not permitted inside historical buildings or aboard Friendship.
