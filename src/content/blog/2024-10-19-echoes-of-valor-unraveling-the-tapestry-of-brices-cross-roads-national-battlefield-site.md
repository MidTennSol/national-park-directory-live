## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

The Battle of Brice's Crossroads took place on June 10, 1864. A strategically important victory for Confederate Major General Nathan Bedford Forrest over Union Brigadier General Samuel D. Sturgis changed the dynamics of the entire Civil War by ensuring critical supplies reached Southern forces.

Today, the battlefield site encompasses ten acres, showcasing a memorial erected to honor those who bravely fought during this tumultuous period. The landscape remains relatively unchanged since that fateful day – quiet fields under vast skies are broken only by patches of woods harboring forgotten stories within their depths.

For those interested in military history or genealogy, Bethany Associate Reformed Presbyterian Church - a present-day sentinel of history - offers detailed records of those engaged in the battle. Additionally, periodic reenactments and special events bring history to life.

## **Visitor Information**

Brices Cross Roads National Battlefield Site is open year-round, but spring and autumn offer the most temperate weather for visitors. The site itself is free to enter, however guided tours are available at a nominal charge.

There are no food or drink facilities on site, so visitors are encouraged to pack their own refreshments. Restrooms are located near the visitor center which also provides maps and additional historical information.
