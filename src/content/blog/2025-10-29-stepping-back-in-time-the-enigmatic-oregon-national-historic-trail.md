## **Introduction**

## **Historical/Cultural/Geographical Background**

Our story begins in 1841 when wagons first rolled westward from Independence, Missouri along a worn Native American trading path. Over 400,000 emigrants braved this treacherous route to seek fortunes in fur trading or fertile lands in the Pacific Northwest. Today's trail boasts impressive geographical diversity; from Kansas City's lush grasslands to Wyoming's stark high desert plateaus; from Idaho’s craggy mountains to Oregon's verdant valleys. This pathway whispers stories of human resilience resonating within cultural landmarks such as Chimney Rock (Nebraska), Fort Laramie (Wyoming), or The Dalles (Oregon).

## **Activities Guide**

The journey on this historic trail can be as varied as your interests: tracing wagon rut remains at Guernsey State Park (Wyoming), following pioneer footsteps at Three Islands Crossing (Idaho), or merely letting serenity take over while observing jaw-dropping vistas atop Flagstaff Hill at Baker County (Oregon). The four Interpretive Centers located strategically across Missouri, Nebraska, Idaho, and Oregon house detailed exhibits and interactive experiences.
