## **Introduction**
Welcome fellow wanderers! Today, we're casting off towards a historical thrill ride along the Captain John Smith Chesapeake National Historic Trail. Traversing six states - Baltimore, Virginia, Maryland, Delaware, Washington D.C., Pennsylvania, and New York - this 3,000-mile-long waterway offers visitors a rich tapestry of culture, history, and natural beauty to explore.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

More than just scenic waterways and lush landscapes; these trails tell a narrative that dates back over four centuries. In 1607-1609 Captain John Smith led two voyages throughout the Chesapeake Bay region to map its vast expanse. Encountering diverse cultures of Native American tribes and witnessing nature in its unspoiled form are stories still being told today through every rustling leaf and whispering wave along this historic trail.

The course threads not only through history but also geology. The unique blend of saltwater from the Atlantic Ocean and freshwater from rivers creates an unusual ecosystem with diverse flora and fauna skillfully adapted to live in this brackish environment.

## **Activities Guide**

With such extensive coverage across multiple states, there are countless opportunities to engage in exhilarating outdoor activities along this historic trail. Walking in the footsteps of those native tribal people or colonial settlers brings alive history in an incredibly immersive way.

Visitors can access various points along the trail by foot, bike, car or – most traditionally – by boat. Here, you can indulge in bird-watching, fishing, sailing, hiking, canoeing, or simply basking in the panoramic views that the trail provides.

The trail is open year-round, inviting adventurers in every season. Facilities and services vary widely across the trail's expansive reach.
