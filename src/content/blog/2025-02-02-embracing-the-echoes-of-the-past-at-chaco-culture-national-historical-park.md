## **Introduction**
Set amid the ruggedly beautiful New Mexico desert, Chaco Culture National Historical Park serves as a window into the rich past of ancestral Puebloans civilization. Preserving one of North America's most significant archeological areas, it lets you marvel at sweeping panoramas peppered with monumental architectural remnants that stand testament to prehistoric culture and life.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geographical Background**
Established in 1980, this national park provides a unique glimpse into the lives of Chacoan people who once thrived here between AD 850-1250. The intricate network of public buildings they left behind is more significant than anything found elsewhere in the Southwest USA.

The Chaco Canyon was a hub for ceremonies, commerce, and trade routes for surrounding communities. Now, these mysterious ruins - including Pueblo Bonito, Casa Rinconada, and Chetro Ketl - tell tales of complex societies that once flourished under desert skies.

Nestled amidst towering sandstone mesas, deep canyons, and expansive high-desert landscapes characterized by sagebrush and pinon-juniper woodland; this sprawling park spans over 33 thousand acres providing a striking contrast against its blue-sky backdrop.

## **Activities Guide**
Exploring this UNESCO World Heritage site demands more than just sightseeing. Embark on guided tours led by park rangers or self-guided hikes to witness the intricate stone structures up close.

Take advantage of the night sky programs, which unveil a panorama of stars and planets rarely visible elsewhere - a testament to the Chacoan's astronomical prowess. For bird-lovers, don't miss a unique chance to observe over 160 species, including Golden Eagles, Peregrine Falcons, and Sandhill Cranes.

You can also attend various educational programs to learn more about the Chacoan culture or try your hand at photographing ancient ruins against sunset hues for an unforgettable memento.

## **Visitor Information**
The park is open year-round from sunrise to sunset. Keep in mind there are no food services, gas stations or public transportation within the park boundaries; thus, carrying provisions is advisable.

Entry fees vary depending on mode of entrance with detailed information available on their official website. Free entry days are also offered periodically throughout the year.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**
Depending upon what kind of traveler you are, Chaco offers something for everybody.
