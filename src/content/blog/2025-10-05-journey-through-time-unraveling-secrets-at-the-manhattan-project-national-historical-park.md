## **Introduction**
Nestled amid the landscapes of New Mexico, Washington, and Tennessee, lies the epicenter of an epoch-defining tale - The Manhattan Project National Historical Park. Spread over three distinct locations that formed the backbone of America’s nuclear research during World War II – Los Alamos in New Mexico; Hanford in Washington; and Oak Ridge in Tennessee - this park offers visitors a unique lens into one of history's most transformative periods.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
The Manhattan Project marked a crucial juncture in human history that forever changed warfare and politics. It was here, under utmost secrecy and an unprecedented global crisis, that America brought together some of its best minds to create atomic weapons to end World War II.

Each site within the park carries significant historical narratives:

- Los Alamos acted as the project's secretive headquarters where theoretical work transformed into practical application.
 
- Hanford served as a plutonium production complex, fueling components for bombs deployed during WWII.

- Oak Ridge provided enriched uranium required for bomb-making through electromagnetic separation process.

Preserving these sites aims to confront questions about legacy, ethics, and impact intertwined with technological innovation and war-time exigencies.

## **Activities Guide**
Touring each site promises an enriching experience:

- In Los Alamos, start by visiting the Bradbury Science Museum followed by a walk through Bathtub Row exploring wartime habitations.

- At Hanford Site, guided tours take you through B Reactor – the world’s first large-scale nuclear reactor and a significant historical touchstone.

- Oak Ridge offers bus tours introducing you to key areas including the X-10 Graphite Reactor, Y-12 New Hope Center, and the city's wartime history.

Be sure to participate in talks, films, and junior ranger programs offered by knowledgeable park rangers. 

## **Visitor Information**
The Manhattan Project National Historical Park is open year-round but specific sites have varying schedules:

- The Los Alamos visitor center operates Wednesday-Sunday from 9:00 AM–5:00 PM. 

- Hanford’s B Reactor tours require reservations and run seasonally between April and October.

- For the Oak Ridge site, guided tours are offered from March to November.
