## **Introduction**
Immerse yourself in the shadowy dimensions of one of America’s greatest literary minds at the Edgar Allan Poe National Historic Site in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. This inconspicuous red-brick building is more than just a house; it's a tangible link to the past and an intimate peek into Poe's tumultuous life and genius. ## **Historical/Cultural/Geographical Background**
This three-story brick house on North 7th street was once home to Edgar Allan Poe, his wife Virginia, and their feline companion Catterina. It was during his residence here between 1843-1844 that several of his famous works like "The Gold Bug," "The Black Cat," and "A Tale-Tell Heart" were conceived, forever intertwining the legacy of this house with the author's gloomy imagination.

Philadelphia played a crucial role in shaping Poe's career. During his stay here, the city teemed with printers and publishers molding it into America's literary epicenter. 

Today, the site preserves the author's only surviving residence in Philadelphia, featuring furnished rooms decorated according to descriptions from Poe’s tales - making it not just a museum but a physical embodiment of his chilling fiction.

## **Activities Guide**
The self-guided tour begins at the visitor center where you can watch a brief introductory film on Poe's life and literary career. After soaking in the eerie ambiance, you can explore the house with its preserved sitting room, bedroom, and basement that inspired "The Black Cat". The highlight of your visit might be the 'Poe Reading Room,' where you could spend hours delving into his works amidst timeless tranquility.

Occasionally, live readings of Poe’s work and special events themed around his life and stories are held here - adding an extra layer of vividness to your visit.

## **Visitor Information**
The Edgar Allan Poe National Historic Site is open Friday to Sunday from 9 AM to 12 PM and 1 PM to 5 PM. Admission is free, but donations are encouraged to help maintain this historic space. It’s important to note that due to its historical nature, only the first floor is wheelchair accessible. Parking around the site can get tricky over weekends, so it's advisable to use public transport or ride-sharing services.
