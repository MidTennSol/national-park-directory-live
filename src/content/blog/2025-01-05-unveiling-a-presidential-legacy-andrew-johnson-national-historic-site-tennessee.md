## **Introduction**

Immersed in historical significance and peppered with remnants of a bygone era, the **Andrew Johnson National Historic Site** holds more than just memories within its hallowed grounds. Located in Greeneville, Tennessee, this unique national park is dedicated to preserving the legacy of America's 17th President, Andrew Johnson - a man known for his strife-ridden presidency following Abraham Lincoln's assassination and remarkable journey from tailor to our Nation’s highest office.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

Born to poverty-stricken parents, **Andrew Johnson's life began without much promise**. His apprenticeship as a tailor set him on an improbable path that led him all the way to the presidency – only to face impeachment shortly thereafter. This site encompasses three sections: The Visitor Complex which includes his tailor shop; Andrew Johnson’s Homestead where he lived before and after his presidency; and Andrew Johnson’s gravesite on Monument Hill.

The original Tailor Shop functions now as a Visitor Center and museum showcasing artefacts from Johnson's life. It offers films providing insights into his rather tumultuous political career.

The homestead on Main Street gives visitors an intimate look into the private life of Andrew Johnson. Restored to its appearance during his presidency in 1865, this home houses personal belongings and period furniture creating an immersive experience into the past.

Lastly stands Monument Hill where President Johnson rests beside his family enclosed by patriotic emblems inspiring a sense of solemn reverence.

## **Activities Guide**

The Andrew Johnson National Historic Site offers **multiple avenues to explore the life and times of the 17th President**. Begin at the Visitor’s Center, housed in Johnson's original tailor shop, where you can watch an informative 14-minute film about his life. Guided tours are available for his homestead offering insights into both his personal life and political status.

Walk through history in downtown Greeneville with its preserved structures featuring significant landmarks from Johnson’s time. For those seeking serenity, visit Monument Hill – a silent testament to Johnson's legacy providing panoramic views of Greeneville.

## **Visitor Information**

The park is open year-round except Thanksgiving Day, December 25th, and January 1st. Admission is free but tour tickets for the Homestead need to be collected from the Visitor’s Center due to limited capacity. Visitor facilities include restrooms, bookstore and picnic areas.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**

For families, the interactive Junior Ranger Program is a great way for young ones to learn about history while having fun. As an accessible site, mobility-impaired visitors can access all areas using ramps and lifts provided. The homestead tour does involve stair climbing though alternatives are available upon request.

History enthusiasts will find the guided tours particularly enriching thanks to knowledgeable park rangers who bring to life Andrew Johnson's era with vivid narrations.

## **Regional Context**

Situated amidst rolling hills, Greeneville is Tennessee's second oldest town rich in heritage. Besides exploring Andrew Johnson National Historic Site, visitors can enjoy scenic drives around Cherokee National Forest nearby or indulge in local Tennessee cuisine famous for barbecued meats and southern comfort dishes.
