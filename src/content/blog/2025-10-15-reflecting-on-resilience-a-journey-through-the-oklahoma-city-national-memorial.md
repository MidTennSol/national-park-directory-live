## **Introduction**
Welcome to a journey that will leave an indelible imprint on your heart. The Oklahoma City National Memorial is unlike any other national park you may have visited. Nestled in the heart of downtown Oklahoma City, it serves as a stirring reminder of resilience woven into tragic loss. As we steer you through this journey, remember - this is not just a guide; it's a tribute to hope persisting against all odds.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
The edifice was built to honor those affected by the devastating bombing of Alfred P. Murrah Federal Building on April 19, 1995. It pays homage to the 168 lives tragically lost and thousands affected by one of the most catastrophic events in American history. The symbolism embedded within each monument speaks volumes about pain, recovery, strength, and unity.

## **Activities Guide**
While there aren't traditional recreational activities at this memorial park, you are embarking upon an enlightening exploration that combines stark history with uplifting symbols of physical and emotional resilience.

The Outdoor Symbolic Memorial is open 24 hours a day and requires no admission fee for visitors to experience the powerful silence of the Reflecting Pool and the Field of Empty Chairs, each representing a life lost in this tragic event.

Inside, the Memorial Museum awaits you. Embark on a self-guided tour which leads you through the narrative of that devastating April day from pre-incident, to moment of impact, rescue efforts, and finally global healing and response. Immerse yourself in exhibits such as 'Investigation and Justice,' 'Gallery of Honor' or 'Survivor's Wall.'

## **Visitor Information**
The Outdoor Symbolic Memorial is open around-the-clock every day. However, the Memorial Museum operates Tuesday through Saturday from 9 am to 5 pm and Sunday from noon until 5 pm (closed on Mondays). Tickets can be purchased online or upon arrival. Seniors, students, military personnel are eligible for discounted rates.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**
For those unable or unwilling to walk long distances, wheelchairs may be rented from the museum, free of charge. Families with children should consider preparing younger ones for the somber tone of this park; an age-appropriate context talk before visiting could prove helpful.

Photography is permitted outdoors but not within the museum galleries - please respect this rule. Lastly, remember there are no dining facilities on site so plan your meals accordingly.
