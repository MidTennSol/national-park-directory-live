## **Introduction**

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

Before it was a national park, Wind Cave served as a sacred site for Native Americans. Specifically, the Lakota people considered it their place of origin. In 1881, two brothers discovered Wind Cave when they noticed wind streaming out from a small hole in the earth.

Wind Cave is geologically unique due to its abundance of "boxwork" formations – honeycomb-patterned calcite structures rarely seen in other caves. With over 140 miles of known passages intricately braided into a maze-like system, it provides an unrivaled spelunking experience.

## **Activities Guide**

Whether you're up for hiking or down for delving into the cave’s depths, there's something for everyone at Wind Creek. On surface level, explore over 30 miles of hiking trails through mixed-grass prairie inhabited by bison, elk, and prairie dogs.

For those eager to explore below ground, choose from several guided tours such as The Natural Entrance Tour where you can witness how early explorers traversed the cave system, or The Fairgrounds Tour for an easy exploration of larger rooms and passageways.

## **Visitor Information**

The park is open year-round with varying activities per season. Cave tours are offered daily, except for Thanksgiving, Christmas, and New Year's Day. Before visiting, keep in mind that temperatures inside the cave remain steady at around 53°F which can feel chilly after heat exposure above ground.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**

For families with children, shorter cave tours like the Garden of Eden Tour are recommended as they require less strenuous activity. Seniors or individuals with mobility issues may also opt for this tour considering it includes fewer stairs and overall distance.

Traveling photographers will be particularly drawn to surface-level wildlife photography opportunities – make sure your camera is ready at dawn and dusk when animals are most active!

## **Regional Context**

Wind Cave National Park sits nestled among other unique South Dakota attractions such as Mount Rushmore National Memorial (only an hour's drive away), Custer State Park - renowned for its herds of wild buffalo, and Crazy Horse Memorial - the world's largest mountain carving in progress.
