## **Introduction**

There is a place where past echoes reverberate through the corridors of time, where majestic walls whisper tales of courageous men and women who struggled for freedom during America's fight for Independence. This remarkable landmark, brimming with rich historical narratives and scenic beauty, is located at the very heart of Rome, New York – welcome to the Fort Stanwix National Monument.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

Constructed originally by the British in 1758 during the French and Indian War, Fort Stanwix earned its claim to fame during the Revolutionary War when its successful defense prevented British forces from splitting the Colonies in half. Nearly forgotten after wars end, it was rebuilt as a National Historic Site in 1935 to honor its vital contribution to American independence. Today's replica fort pays homage to this turbulent era while providing an immersive education experience.

Beyond its military history though, Fort Stanwix profoundly carries deep connections with Native American people. It served as a trading post for several Indigenous nations long before European settlers arrived. The Treaty of Fort Stanwix in 1768 forever reshaped Iroquois territory boundaries.

Geologically, this region offers fascinating terrain formed due to glaciation thousands of years ago. The Mohawk River valley that flows nearby allowed early trade among Indigenous tribes and later eased westward expansion during colonial times.

## **Activities Guide**

Visitors are drawn into another world at Fort Stanwix – stepping across wooden drawbridges over earth ramparts and under towering timber bastions. The fort houses several restored buildings like the Officer's Quarters, Enlisted Men's Barracks, and a powder magazine, each brimming with historical artifacts.

Outside the fort walls, explore trails along the Mohawk River, part of larger Erie Canalway National Heritage Corridor. Winter offers opportunities for cross-country skiing or snowshoeing.

## **Visitor Information**

The Fort Stanwix National Monument grounds are open year-round from 9 am to 5 pm except on Thanksgiving Day, Christmas Day, and New Year’s Day when it is closed. While there is no entrance fee, donations are appreciated. Ample free parking is available adjacent to the Visitor Center at North James and Dominick Streets. Restrooms and picnic facilities are located onsite.
