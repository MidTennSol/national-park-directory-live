## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
Constructed in 1759 as a wedding gift for John Vassall Jr., the house became pivotal during the Revolutionary War when General Washington utilized it as his headquarters from 1775-1776. Afterward, Andrew Craigie, Apothecary General to the Continental Army, bought it.
The rich layers of history came alive when Henry Wadsworth Longfellow moved in after his wedding in 1843. It was within these walls he crafted timeless poems like "Evangeline" and "The Song of Hiawatha". The home remained within the family until its donation to the National Park Service by Longfellow's descendants in 1972.

Comprising Georgian architecture and surrounded by picturesque gardens ornamented with historical flora, this magnificent structure holds an aura both imposing and inviting.
