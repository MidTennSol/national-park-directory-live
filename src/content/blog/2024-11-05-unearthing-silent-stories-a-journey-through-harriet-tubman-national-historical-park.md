## **Introduction**

Embark on an unforgettable journey into America’s poignant past as you step onto the soil of Harriet Tubman National Historical Park in Auburn, New York. Considered sacred ground, this is where Harriet Tubman - a renowned abolitionist and conductor of the Underground Railroad - found peace after a lifetime marked by struggle and courage. Enveloped by a history both heartrending and triumphant, this park invites all to partake in its quiet reflection.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

Harriet Tubman's extraordinary life stands as one of the most significant chapters in American history. As the 'Moses of her people,' she braved unimaginable danger to lead hundreds of slaves out of bondage via the clandestine network known as the Underground Railroad.

The park itself encompasses several significant sites including Tubman’s residence, the Home for The Aged that she established, and the Thompson Memorial African Methodist Episcopal Zion Church where she worshipped. Each building resonates with the echoes of her incredible spirit and life mission to uplift those around her.

## **Activities Guide**

Stroll along winding paths that harken back to times long past while indulging in self-guided or ranger-led tours. You can explore exhibits showcasing personal artifacts and discover detailed accounts about Tubman's sacrifices at the Visitor Center’s Museum Exhibit – "Harriet Tubman: From Slavery, To Freedom." 

For history enthusiasts seeking deeper understanding, attend enlightening talks presented by knowledgeable rangers who passionately unfurl tales from yesteryears. Though small, this park is a treasure trove for those interested in the intersection of history, culture, and social justice.

## **Visitor Information**

The park opens daily from 10 am-4 pm except on major holidays. There is no admission fee, but donations are always appreciated.
