## **Introduction**
In the heartland of Crow Agency, Montana radiates an extraordinary landmark that captures a pivotal moment in American history - the Little Bighorn Battlefield National Monument. Stretching over 765 acres, this national treasure immortalizes the site where one of the most infamous armed conflicts between Native Americans and US Government forces unraveled—the Battle of Little Bighorn. This monument is not just a site but a rich tapestry weaving together stories of courage, conflict, and cultural exchange that date back to June 25-26, 1876.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
The Battle of Little Bighorn—also known as Custer's Last Stand—marked an intense conflict in the Great Sioux War. Fought between combined forces of Northern Cheyenne and Lakota tribes against the 7th Cavalry Regiment led by Lieutenant Colonel George Armstrong Custer, this historical battle embodies Native American resistance against U.S. government encroachment on their lands.

Today's visitor will find a landscape surprisingly unchanged from its status in 1876 – rolling grasslands punctuated by coulees, ridges, and bluffs provide breathtaking vistas that honor those who fought on this hallowed ground. The monument echoes with stories of valor conveyed through commemorative markers spread across battle sites.

## **Visitor Information**
The monument during summer (Memorial Day-Labor Day) opens from 8 am till 9 pm while in winter it wraps up by 4:30 pm. Facilities include restrooms, picnic areas, and an on-site bookstore managed by Western National Parks Association.
