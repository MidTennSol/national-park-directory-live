Knife River Indian Villages National Historic Site isn't just a park. It's an unforgettable voyage through time that paints a living portrait of upper Plains Indian life as it was over 500 years ago. Tucked away in the remote landscapes of North Dakota, this historic site stands as an echo of ancient civilization embedded within acres of rolling prairies, rugged hillsides, and winding rivers that color the landscape — a natural museum that plays tribute to our nation’s earliest inhabitants.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

Established in 1974 to preserve historic and archaeological remnants of the agricultural Hidatsa tribes, Knife River holds significant value as one of few places where these northern Plains cultures blossomed around A.D. 1100-1845. The site spans three villages — Big Hidatsa, Awatixa Xi'e (Lower Hidatsa), and Sakakawea Village — each offering unique insights into the lives of its early residents. Earthlodge ruins dot the landscape, remnants from an era when some villages had populations surpassing many European cities.

The geology further shapes this remarkable tapestry with siltstone cliffs cutting dramatic silhouettes against North Dakota’s endless sky. The park is uniquely situated on upland prairie with river bottom forests creating diverse flora and fauna habitats.

## **Visitor Information**

Knife River Indian Villages National Historic Site is open year-round with the Visitor Center operating from 9:00am-4:30pm daily (closed on Thanksgiving Day, Christmas Day, and New Year's Day). Entrance is free with potential fees for special events. 

The center provides enlightening exhibits about Hidatsa culture and history. Visitors can view artifacts and participate in interactive experiences designed for all ages. Wheelchair accessibility is also available.
