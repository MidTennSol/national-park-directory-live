## **Introduction**
Peering high above the rolling prairie surrounding the Black Hills, Devils Tower is a sight that captures awe and wonder. It stands as an ancient sentinel, its unique structure challenging the very fabric of Wyoming’s typical landscape. This imposing monolith has become a captivating draw for tourists, historians, geologists, and climbers alike; each enthralled by its grandness. As you approach it across a dusky landscape where bison graze lazily under cottonwood trees, you begin to sense why Native American folklore reveres this place so deeply.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
Devils Tower is not just a striking geographical feature; it's also steeped in cultural significance for over 20 different Native American tribes who consider it sacred. Known by many names such as "Bear Lodge," "Bear's Tipi," or "Brown Buffalo Horn," oral histories depict this place as central to their spiritual traditions.

The formation of this geological marvel remains a mystery stoking scientific intrigue. Most agree that it was formed by molten magma intrusion about 40 million years ago which then solidified into phonolite porphyry - a rare type of igneous rock known for its columnar joints giving Devils Tower its eerie, grooved facade. Erosion eventually unveiled its true majesty – standing tall at 867 feet from base to summit.

## **Activities Guide**
Visitors have a myriad of ways to enjoy Devils Tower. For a leisurely exploration, take the 1.3-mile Tower Trail circling the base where you can observe the tower’s vertical striations up close and perhaps glimpse climbers inching their way up.

For adrenaline seekers, climbing Devils Tower is an ultimate goal. However, it is not for the inexperienced and requires registration with the park service beforehand.

## **Visitor Information**
The monument is open year-round except for Thanksgiving Day, December 25th, and January 1st. Entrance fees are $25 per vehicle or $20 per motorcycle. Annual passes are available for avid visitors at $45.

The visitor center endeavors to enhance your understanding of this national treasure by offering educational exhibits about its history, geology, and cultural significance. Amenities such as picnic areas, campgrounds (including Belle Fourche River Campground), and restrooms facilitate visitor comfort during your trip.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**
Families will enjoy nature walks spotting prairie dogs or participating in popular ranger-led programs like interpretive talks and nighttime stargazing events.

For seniors or those with mobility issues, the Circle of Sacred Smoke Sculpture invites quiet contemplation without strenuous activity involved. Young couples might find pleasure in capturing unforgettable sunset shots when the tower blushes with hues of orange and red.

Climbers must respect the voluntary June climbing closure to honor Native American ceremonies taking place near Devils Tower during this month - an expression of respect towards local indigenous cultures.

## **Regional Context**
Devils Tower National Monument lies just over an hour's drive from Rapid City in South Dakota – making it an accessible day-trip if you're visiting Mount Rushmore or Badlands National Park in that region.
