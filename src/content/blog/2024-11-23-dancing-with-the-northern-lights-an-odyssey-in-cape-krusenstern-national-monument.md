The layers of Cape Krusenstern reveal narratives far older than any written book. Here, 114 beach ridges showcase unique chronicles etched into stone by earth and ice through millennia. Yet Cape Krusenstern isn’t just about geology. The Inupiat people found fertile hunting grounds here around 4000 B.C., leaving behind artifacts that are discovered even today by archaeologists. ## **Visitor Information**

The National Park Service does not charge an entry fee for Cape Krusenstern National Monument. However, it is remotely located and often only accessible via air taxi services from Kotzebue. Prior arrangements should be made for accommodation as there are no public facilities or lodgings within the park itself.

During winter months, weather conditions can be extremely harsh, underlining the importance of carefully planned visits. Local rangers can provide essential guidance and safety information tailored to individual needs and activities.
