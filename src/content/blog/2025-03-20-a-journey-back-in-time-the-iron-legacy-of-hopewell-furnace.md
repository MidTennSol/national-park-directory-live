## **Introduction**
Welcome to a unique exploration of one of America’s earliest and most significant industrial fronts - Hopewell Furnace National Historic Site. Nestled in the verdant landscapes of Eastern Pennsylvania, this historic site takes you on a journey through time to an era when America was molding its industrial foundations. After being active for over 113 years (1771-1883), today, Hopewell continues to portray the life and legacy of early American settlers through ongoing restoration efforts that showcase craft demonstrations, story-telling sessions, and engaging activities. 

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background** 
Hopewell Furnace showcases an early American landscape dotted with trees, fields, watercourses, and structures – all infused with historical relevance. Surrounded by French Creek State Park, it protects the remnants of an 18th and 19th-century iron plantation. This village-like industry was borne out of raw innovation and hard work, playing a crucial role in turning wrought iron into war necessities during the Revolutionary War as well as routinely-produced goods. Furthermore, you can explore 'big house', charcoal barns, workers’ cabins or anthracite furnaces which retain their original character, detailing more about working conditions, social status divisions and life cycle within early American industries.

## **Visitor Information**
Entrance to Hopewell Furnace is free throughout the year and it operates between 9 AM to 5 PM from Wednesday to Sunday. However, during winter from November through April, keep in mind that some buildings may be closed due to weather conditions.
