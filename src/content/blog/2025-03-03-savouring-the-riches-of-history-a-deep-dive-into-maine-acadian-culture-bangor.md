## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

The story of Maine's Acadian culture begins with French-Canadian settlers who migrated to the maritimes in the 17th century. Known as Acadians, these resilient people created a flourishing community that survived numerous political upheavals - including The Great Expulsion by British forces in 1755. Many stayed behind or returned years later, persistently clinging to their roots while integrating into new realities.

Maine's geographical features also played a hand in sculpturing this unique culture. Lush forests, bountiful rivers and undulating hills offered resources that guided their survival strategies leading to unusual traditions such as 'Ice Fisheries'.
