## **Introduction**
Set amidst the rolling hills and verdant surroundings of Cornish, New Hampshire, lies an encapsulated world of history, artistic grandeur, and serene beauty - **Saint-Gaudens National Historical Park**. This park is more than just a verdant retreat; it’s a shrine built around the memory of American sculptor Augustus Saint-Gaudens whose vast body of work dramatically transformed American art.

With this post, embrace a journey that bridges nature with history through sweeping lawns, historic buildings, lush orchards, aromatic gardens and magisterial sculptures that dot Saint-Gaudens' hallowed grounds.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
In these tranquil environs spent his last 25 years Augustus Saint-Gaudens (1848 – 1907), one of America's most significant sculptors. Born to Irish immigrants in Dublin but brought up in New York City, his career spanned several decades during which he created over 150 pieces - coins to monuments - articulating narratives from civil war heroes to contemplative religious figures.

Established as a national site in 1964, today's park spans over 148 acres preserving his home - Aspet mansion-, studios and galleries. The grounds ripple with geological wonders such as the ancient metamorphic rock outcroppings visible throughout adding to its natural allure.

## **Activities Guide**
**Saint-Gaudens National Historical Park** presents an ensemble of activities for diverse interests. Art enthusiasts can revel in the galleries housing Saint-Gaudens’ major works, including the Shaw Memorial or the contemplative "Adams" statue.

Nature lovers and birdwatchers can walk along the ¾ mile Ravine Trail, a woodland path boasting magnificent views of Mount Ascutney and an array of local flora and fauna. Guided tours offering historical insight run frequently.

The Summer Concert series comes alive with classical music performances at the park's natural amphitheater, while artists-in-residence present workshops throughout the season. Special events such as sculpture exhibits, historic encampments, and curator talks add to its magnetic charm.

## **Visitor Information**
Open year-round, though building access varies by season. In summer (Memorial Day through October), buildings are open daily while winter sees limited availability. The entrance fee is $10 per person for ages 16 and older.

Visitors are encouraged to check their website for updated information on pandemic-related restrictions. The site is wheelchair accessible with a selection of braille transcriptions available.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**
This national gem caters to various visitors from families to scholars:

1. For Families: Engage your children with junior ranger programs or explore 'Little Studio', a hands-on discovery room.
2. For Art Lovers: Take it slow; these works demand time to absorb their intricate narratives.
3. For History Buffs: Plan your visit around special historical events like Civil War Encampments.
4. For Outdoor Enthusiasts: Pack for a picnic; there are several picturesque spots to enjoy a leisurely meal amidst beauty.
5. For Scholars: A research library houses Saint-Gaudens' written materials providing insightful peeks into his life and work.

## **Regional Context**
While immersing in Saint-Gaudens’ world draws you in, the surrounding Upper Valley region also promises eclectic attractions. A drive away is the picturesque village of Woodstock and Quechee Gorge, Vermont's deepest gorge known as 'Vermont's Little Grand Canyon.' The Dartmouth College campus too isn't far off if you're inclined to explore more culture and history.

## **Conclusion**
A trip to **Saint-Gaudens National Historical Park** offers a rare blend of art, nature, history, and tranquility unseen elsewhere. With its rich offerings of artistic excellence and engaging activities amidst serene landscapes, it indeed presents an unforgettable journey through time.
