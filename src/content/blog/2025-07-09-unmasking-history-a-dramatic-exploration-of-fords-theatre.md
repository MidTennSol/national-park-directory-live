## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

Ford’s Theatre opened its doors in August 1863. Built by John T. Ford, a theatrical entrepreneur from Baltimore, it initially started as the First Baptist Church. However, after a devastating fire ravaged the original building in 1862 leaving only its brick walls intact, fortune breathed life into these ruins granting them an exciting new destiny - to become a theatre.

But on April 14th, 1865, this thriving stage transformed tragically into an unforgettable murder scene. During a comedy play "Our American Cousin," President Lincoln was shot by John Wilkes Booth. He died the next morning marking an everlasting gloom over this lively place.

## **Visitor Information**

The theatre is located at 511 10th St NW in Washington D.C. Do bear in mind that visitation may be subject to pandemic-related restrictions or closures, so it's wise to check their official website before you plan your visit.

Tickets range from $3 for museum-only access to $27.50 if you decide to enjoy a historical walking tour. They also provide free entry for anyone under five years old or holders of National Parks and Federal Recreation Lands passes.
