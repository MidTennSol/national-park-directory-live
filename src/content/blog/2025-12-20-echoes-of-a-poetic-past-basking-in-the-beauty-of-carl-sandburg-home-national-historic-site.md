## **Introduction**

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
Designated as a National Historic Site in 1968, three years after Carl Sandburg's death at age 89—the sprawling 264-acre park encapsulates his life's work. Affectionately referred to as "the People's Poet," Sandburg passionately wrote about American society—the laborer's struggles, urban dynamics, political concerns—and his verses resonated deeply with the masses.

Equally impressive are the geological features that lure avid nature lovers. The park offers a slice of Blue Ridge Mountains' rugged beauty right in Asheville's backyard—a perfect haven for geology enthusiasts to explore rock formations dating back millions of years!

## **Activities Guide**
Immerse yourself in various activities that cater to different interests. Take a guided tour inside Sandburg’s antique-filled house—see his typewriter on his original desk next to piles of books that bestow a glimpse into his world. 

For those with a penchant for the great outdoors, choose between five tranquil miles of hiking trails. Wander through serene woodlands, stride along lush meadows, or take a leisurely stroll by the peaceful Front Lake—a cherished spot where Sandburg penned some of his most famous works.

## **Visitor Information**
The park is open daily from 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM except on Thanksgiving, Christmas, and New Year's Day. Admission is free, though house tours come with a nominal fee ($8 for adults and $5 for seniors). The park also offers First Amendment spaces that can be reserved for public expressions.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**
Young families might want to start with the Junior Ranger program—an interactive way for kids to learn about Sandburg's life and legacy while exploring nature.

History buffs should check out special events such as Memorial Day Weekend Folk Music Festival—a celebration entrenched in Sandburg’s love for American folk music traditions.

Nature enthusiasts? Don’t forget to pack binoculars! Connemara surprises birdwatchers with various species including Carolina Chickadees, Red-Tailed Hawks, and Great Blue Herons!
