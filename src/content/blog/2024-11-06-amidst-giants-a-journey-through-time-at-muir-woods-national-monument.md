## **Introduction**
Step into the towering shadows of majestic giants at Muir Woods National Monument. Situated a stone's throw away from bustling San Francisco, this ethereal woodland presents an ancient untouched world awaiting exploration. Time seems to stand still under these wise old redwoods, creating a tranquil oasis amid California’s rapidly evolving landscape. Within its quiet glades and leaf-carpeted paths, discover the power of nature and our deep connections to it.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
Muir Woods' story begins millions of years ago with the emergence of these ageless coast redwood trees (Sequoia sempervirens), some reaching up to 258 feet high - equivalent to a 23-story building! As one of the planet's oldest living species, they offer a unique window into Earth's prehistoric times.

The park owes its preservation to business mogul William Kent and his wife Elizabeth Thacher Kent. Faced with imminent logging threats in the early 1900s, the Kents purchased land to protect these primordial trees. In gratitude for their conservation efforts, President Theodore Roosevelt declared it a National Monument in 1908, taking William Kent’s suggestion to name it after renowned naturalist John Muir.

## **Activities Guide**

Birdwatchers will revel in the opportunity to spot over 50 species of birds including owls, woodpeckers and hawks. Also look out for fauna such as black-tailed deer, raccoons or possibly a bobcat! ## **Visitor Information**
Muir Woods is open year-round, with operating hours varying by season. Reservations are required for parking and shuttle services due to limited availability. The entrance fee stands at $15 per adult while those under 16 enjoy free admission. Be aware that no cellular service is available within Muir Woods - a perfect excuse to switch off from digital distractions.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**
Elderly or physically impaired visitors should stick to the main trail which winds gently through Cathedral Grove and Bohemian Grove. For a strenuous hike, take the Dipsea trail leading you outside the dense forest onto sunnier chaparral terrain affording sweeping views.

Younger visitors will enjoy becoming Junior Rangers by participating in an adventure-filled activity guide offered by park rangers. For those seeking tranquility, early mornings or late afternoons during weekdays provide fewer crowds.
