## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

The park stands testament to three illustrious families—the Marshes, Billings, and Rockefellers—each leaving indelible imprints on American conservation history. The property began as George Perkins Marsh’s childhood home in 1805; this space ignited his revolutionary ideas that later shaped the global conservation movement.

Equally impressive was Frederick Billings. Inspired by Marsh's writings, he embarked on pioneering reforestation efforts and progressive dairy farming practices right here in Woodstock.

Finally came the Rockefeller family who embellished the estate with exquisite landscaping while preserving historical artifacts thereby cementing its importance as a living museum of land stewardship.

From cultural aspect to geological wonders - Mount Tom stands tall as an iconic figure within the park boundaries showcasing Vermont’s quintessential landscape – dense woodlands juxtaposed against open fields – offering panoramic vistas that leave visitors mesmerized.

## **Activities Guide**

While every corner sings paeans to conservation history, there's more to Marsh-Billings-Rockefeller Park than just historical exploration. A variety of engaging activities await eager explorers, all wrapped in the cloak of nature.

Navigate through the lush woodland on a guided tour and discover the secrets whispered by century-old trees. Hike on over 20 miles of carriage roads and trails traversing breathtaking landscapes.
