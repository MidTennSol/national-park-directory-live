## **Introduction**
Brushing aside partisan politics and time-bound conventions, America's First Ladies have consistently represented both grace and mettle. In Canton, Ohio lies a timeless tribute to these stalwart women — The First Ladies National Historic Site. This immersive chronicle hosts an enlightening journey across two buildings - The Education Center and The Saxton House – each brimming with invaluable artifacts, tales of triumph, and fascinating trivia about the influential wives of America's Commanders-in-Chief.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geographical Background**
The narrative of our nation’s first ladies unfolded against diverse backdrops from sprawling plantations to bustling cities. It is fitting then that their collective legacy finds a home not in one building but two - each bearing its unique cultural significance. 

The Saxton House was the childhood home of Ida McKinley (the 25th First Lady). It provides an intimate look into her life before entering The White House. Next door, the Education Center offers expansive exhibits tracing all first ladies' impact over centuries. 

This site serves as a beacon illuminating their pivotal roles from managing social functions to spearheading policy changes. Their work contributed significantly to shaping public opinion about gender roles in political life.

## **Activities Guide**
To ensure your visit captures every facet this national historic site has to offer; there are several mandatory stops on your itinerary. Begin at the Education Center for insightful exhibits that chronicle the evolution of First Ladies' roles and responsibilities, from Martha Washington to Jill Biden. Learn about their incredible contributions to issues close to their hearts, from literacy to mental health care.

## **Visitor Information**
Open Tuesdays through Saturdays from 9 am - 4 pm with free admission, this primer in political history is accessible for all. Guided tours are available but must be reserved in advance due to limited capacity.

1. **Is photography allowed inside the buildings?**
Yes, photography is allowed for personal use only. However, the use of tripods and flash is prohibited.

2. **Are there facilities for disabled visitors?**
Yes, both the Education Center and Saxton House are wheelchair accessible. 

3. **Is there a gift shop on-site?**
There's a gift shop at the Education Center where you can purchase books, postcards, and memorabilia related to America’s First Ladies.

4. **Can this site be booked for private events?**
No, private events aren’t permitted at this National Historic Site.

5. **Do they offer any special programs throughout the year?**
Yes, they host educational programming which varies seasonally as well as on special occasions tied to historical events or birthdays of First Ladies.
