## **Introduction** 

Located near Frederick, Maryland, Monocacy National Battlefield is an expanse where the past meets the present. The echoes of cannons resound among verdant fields, concealing the marked intensity of historical events beneath their peaceful veneer. As your footprints join those left by soldiers centuries ago, time seems to fold back onto itself. 

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

Monocacy National Battlefield commemorates the historic Battle of Monocacy during the American Civil War. On July 9, 1864, Confederate forces clashed against Union troops in a desperate attempt to capture Washington D.C. Though considered a Union defeat, it bought critical time for Northern defenses to stiffen around the nation's capital.

The park comprises significant landmarks such as Worthington Farmhouse & Ford, Thomas Farm, Best Farm; each carrying a rich narrative steeped in history. Limestone-rich soils nurture fertile farmlands that bear testament to centuries-old agricultural practices in this region.

## **Activities Guide**

While primarily renowned for its historical context, there's more than meets the eye at Monocacy National Battlefield. With over five hiking trails winding across stunning terrain, showcasing vibrant flora and fauna along Brush Creek and Monocacy River—it offers abundant opportunities for outdoor enthusiasts.

Visit the Visitor Center for interpretive exhibits offering detailed historical insights or view a short film recounting the Battle of Monocacy experiences. History buffs will love participating in battlefield tours led by park rangers, offering a vivid perspective of the historic incident.

## **Visitor Information**

The park is open year-round from sunrise to sunset, with the Visitor Center operational Wednesday through Monday, 9 AM to 5 PM. The center offers restrooms and a bookstore operated by Eastern National. Parking is available at each major site. Entry is free for everyone.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**

For families: Pack a picnic and unwind by Monocacy River or hike easy trails like Brookes Hill Loop.

For photographers: Capture stunning sunsets over lush fields or wildlife in action along Brush Creek.

For history enthusiasts: Participate in ranger-led interpretive programs for an immersive experience.

For bird watchers: Be on the lookout for species like American Kestrel, Eastern Meadowlark, and Field Sparrow.
