## **Introduction**

Welcome fellow time travelers to a place where echoes still linger from a revolutionary era. Nestled among pines and towering oaks, just north of Wilmington, North Carolina lies the overlooked gem that is **Moores Creek National Battlefield**. As you step onto this hallowed ground, you're stepping back into 1776 when courageous patriots fought against loyalist forces in what proved a decisive victory in favor of American Independence.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

The Battle of Moore's Creek Bridge occurred on February 27, 1776. This land now commemorates this key event with several walking trails guiding visitors through colonial-era plantation fields and swamplands. Engage with history at interpretive stations dotted along each trail, revealing secrets from both geological ages ago and moments frozen in time from the Revolutionary war.

The battlefield tells a tale of brave regulators protecting their bridge against an incursion by Loyalists armed with broadswords. The Patriots' victory here was vital; it squashed British hope for an easy win in the South and fueled growing sentiment for independence.

## **Activities Guide**

Walking is indeed the best way to absorb the park’s somber history and serene natural beauty. The History Trail is a one-mile loop running beside moores creek where remains of the patriot-built earthen fort can be seen far across on high ground. The Tarheel trail takes you through wetlands rich with diverse flora that flourishes under North Carolinean weather, offering an exciting nature experience.

## **Visitor Information**

Moores Creek National Battlefield is readily accessible with free admission all year round. The Visitor center, where you can find informative exhibits and a brief film about the battle, typically operates from 9 am to 5 pm. However, always check the National Park Service website for most updated operating hours especially considering COVID-related restrictions.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**

Families with small children will appreciate the Junior Ranger Program that makes learning fun. History buffs should not miss out on guided tours to delve deeper into the Battle's pivotal significance. Nature lovers, remember to bring binoculars; this park is home to over 150 bird species!

Moreover, comfortable footwear is advisable as some parts of trails may get muddy after rainfall.
