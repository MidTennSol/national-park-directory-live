## **Introduction**
Welcome, fellow explorers, to a land defined by its raw beauty and unparalleled historical significance - the Bering Land Bridge National Preserve. Situated in western Alaska, this park covers 2.7 million acres of tundra, mountains, lakes and lava flows. A region replete with ice-age fossils and archaeological remains, it's a gateway to our continent's early human history.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
Dating back thousands of years ago during the last Ice Age, this area served as a crucial land bridge between Asia and North America. Migrants traversed this frozen expanse, marking the first peopling of North America. Today, this bridge is submerged beneath the frigid waters of Bering Strait.

This preserve is also steeped in geological fascination as it harbors remnants of volcanic activity. Here you can see rugged landscapes formed by ancient lava flows from Imuruk Volcanic Field and marvel at the Lost Jim Lava Flow – an endless blackened landscape covering nearly 88 square miles.

The land has never lost its spiritual significance for indigenous communities either. The Inupiaq Eskimo people have inhabited these lands for millenniums drawing life from marine resources such as seals, whales and fish.

## **Activities Guide**
The untamed wilderness offers a broad range of activities tailored to suit diverse interests. If solitude appeals to you more than anything else, then here you'll find it in abundance while hiking across open tundra or exploring the preserve's 2,600 miles of rivers and streams by kayak.

For wildlife enthusiasts, Birding is a popular activity with over 170 species to spot. And if you're prepared for the challenging weather conditions, winter activities such as dog-sledding, snowmobiling, and ice-fishing await you on icy landscapes blanketed in deep snow.

## **Visitor Information**
Due to its remote location, visiting Bering Land Bridge National Preserve requires meticulous planning. The park headquarters and visitor center is located in Nome and provides essential information about safety guidelines and recent wildlife sightings. There are no roads leading directly into the preserve; access is primarily through small aircraft charters or boat services that operate seasonally.

Camping is allowed throughout the preserve, but facilities are primitive at best. As such, visitors should pack everything they need for survival in variable weather conditions.
