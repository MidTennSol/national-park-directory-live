## **Introduction**

Nestled amidst the heart of the Pacific Northwest, a mere three-hour drive from Seattle, lies the stunningly beautiful and vastly underrated – North Cascades National Park. It’s a verdant wonderland laden with glacier-crowned mountains, emerald green lakes, dense forests and a myriad of wildlife; an indisputable treasure trove for nature lovers and adventure enthusiasts alike.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

The park's geological story unfurls over millions of years - a tale sculpted by ice age glaciers that carved out valleys and left behind dramatic ridgelines. Historically, this land was home to various Native American tribes like the Nlaka'pamux (formerly known as Thompson) and Chelan.

In 1968, recognizing its ecological significance and natural beauty, it was christened as a National Park by President Lyndon B. Johnson. Today, the park serves as a living museum manifesting relics of Native American heritage, pioneering mining operations, early homesteading evidence and tales of environmental conservation.

## **Activities Guide**

North Cascades offers activities aplenty throughout all seasons. In warmer months hike along some 400 miles of trails encompassing lush wildflower meadows or kayak across Diablo Lake’s turquoise waters surrounded by awe-inspiring mountain vistas. Birdwatchers will delight in spotting majestic Bald Eagles or elusive Spotted Owls amidst old-growth forests.

Winter transforms the park into a frosted paradise, perfect for snowshoeing or cross-country skiing. Remember to pack your camera - every corner teems with photographic opportunities.

## **Visitor Information**

The park is open year-round and entrance is free! However, some areas may close due to seasonal conditions. Visitor centers such as North Cascades Visitor Center in Newhalem and Golden West Visitor Center at Stehekin provide maps, trail information and educational exhibits.

Camping enthusiasts have over 140 sites to choose from, but note that backcountry camping requires a permit obtained by lottery system.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**

Families will love the Junior Ranger Program where kids can engage in activities centered around the park’s unique ecosystem. For visitors with mobility limitations, many visitor centers and certain trails are ADA-accessible. Seniors should remember to take advantage of America’s Beautiful Senior Pass for discounts.

Photographers: prepare for early morning hikes to capture the enchanting sunrise hues over rugged mountain peaks.

## **Regional Context**

Positioned in Washington State's northwestern region, North Cascades National Park is part of an eco-region known as the ‘Northwest Interior Forest.’ It's an area significant for its extensive old-growth forests and unique alpine environments housing rare species like gray wolves and grizzly bears.

Its location also enables easy access to other prominent regional attractions like Mt. Rainier National Park or Olympic National Park for those desiring extended exploration.
