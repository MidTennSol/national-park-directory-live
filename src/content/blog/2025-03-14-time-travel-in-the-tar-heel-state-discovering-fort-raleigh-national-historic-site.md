## **Introduction**
Welcome to Fort Raleigh National Historic Site – a place where history whispers from every corner and nature puts on her finest display. Located on Roanoke Island in Dare County, North Carolina, this national park is a tantalizing blend of historical intrigue and serene landscapes. Yes, it's here that you'll find traces of England's first New World settlements – home to the poignant tale of the "Lost Colony" – all set amid sprawling meadows, dense maritime forests, and tranquil salt marshes.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
In the annals of American history, few stories are as haunting as that of the Lost Colony. In 1587, English colonists arrived on Roanoke Island under patron Sir Walter Raleigh. They built a fort—later christened Fort Raleigh—marking one of England's earliest attempts to colonize North America. However, they mysteriously disappeared leaving behind only one cryptic clue—"CROATOAN"—carved into a wooden post.

Today, Fort Raleigh is more than just an archaeological site; it’s a living chronicle teeming with glimpses into our past. From reconstructed earthworks to Native American town sites and Freedmen’s colony remnants (a community founded by African Americans after the Civil War), each layer peels back another age for exploration.

## **Activities Guide**
Fort Raleigh offers much for those hungry for historical knowledge or nature engagement alike. Explore the visitor center's exhibits highlighting Roanoke Island's history. Embark on a self-guided tour along the Thomas Hariot Nature Trail, soaking up gorgeous views of marshlands, forests, and wildlife. Visit reconstructed portions of the fort to immerse yourself in the mystery surrounding the vanished colony.

Don’t miss the outdoor drama "The Lost Colony," performed during summer at Waterside Theatre. This enthralling production artfully recounts the story of the ill-fated settlers and adds an exciting dimension to your park experience.

## **Visitor Information**
Fort Raleigh National Historic Site is open year-round. Entry is free, but there may be fees for performances or special events. Park facilities include picnic areas, restrooms, and a gift shop. Remember to check weather conditions before you visit and dress accordingly as much of your time will be out in nature’s embrace.
