## **Introduction**
Rising majestically from turquoise waters, colorful coral reefs kiss the surface while mangrove forests sway gently in wake of a salty sea breeze; welcome to Biscayne National Park. Located just a few miles off the dazzling coastline of Miami, Florida, this national treasure comprises 95% water, making it a unique playground for snorkelers, divers, boaters or anyone looking to connect with nature in an intimate manner.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
Biscayne National Park's rich tapestry is woven from various threads of time – from prehistoric Stiltsville houses that stand like spectral sentinels above aquamarine shallows, to historic lighthouses casting long shadows on emerald islands. Carved out by geological forces over millennia, the park is an amalgam of four distinct ecosystems - vibrant coral reefs teeming with biodiversity, lush mangrove forests cradling shores, verdant islands dotting the horizon and clear blue waterways inviting exploration.

The area played home to many cultures throughout history – indigenous tribes used these waters for sustenance, Bahamian fishermen established communities on local islands and fast-paced Miami continued to develop mere miles away.

## **Activities Guide**
With most of its territory submerged beneath gentle waves, Biscayne presents a myriad of water-based activities. Strap on your snorkeling gear and dive into an underwater wonderland where neon-colored fish flit through rainbow-hued corals. Try scuba diving to explore the eerie remnants of six shipwrecks along the Maritime Heritage Trail.

On the surface, boating, canoeing, and kayaking offer a different perspective of the park. Cast your line into the water for some fishing, or simply soak up sunsets that set the sky aflame whilst floating lazily on blue-green waters.

For landlubbers, there's hiking on Elliott Key - home to lush vegetation and hiking trails. Or soak in history at Boca Chita Key with its picturesque lighthouse.

## **Visitor Information**
The Dante Fascell Visitor Center is where your exploration begins. Here you'll find exhibits about local wildlife, ecosystems and cultural history. The center also hosts lectures, films and ranger-led programs throughout the year.

Boat tours for snorkeling or sightseeing are available from outside vendors or you can launch your own boat at one of two public ramps. Remember, there are no restaurants in the park but picnic areas are available – so pack snacks!

## **Tips for Different Visitors**
Those new to Biscayne should join a ranger-led program to get acquainted with the marine ecosystem and safety measures before venturing out on their own. Seasoned explorers might consider camping under starry skies on Elliot or Boca Chita Keys.

Birdwatchers will delight in migration season (October-April). Fishing enthusiasts will appreciate winter's cooler, windier weather which brings bigger game fish closer to shore.

Visitors looking for tranquility should plan on weekdays or during off-peak times (April-June & September-November).

## **Regional Context**
Biscayne National Park offers a serene contrast to Miami's energetic lifestyle just miles away. Its proximity makes it easy for visitors to combine a trip here with urban attractions like South Beach's art deco architecture, Little Havana’s Latin flavor or Everglades National Park’s raw wilderness.

## **Conclusion**

1. **Is there an entrance fee for the park?**
No, admission to the park is free but boat tours and campsites have fees.
2. **Can I camp on any island in Biscayne?**
Camping is only allowed on Elliott Key and Boca Chita Key.
3. **Are there amenities on the camping islands?**
Boca Chita has restrooms but no drinking water while Elliott Key has both, plus a picnic area with grills.
4. **Can I hire a boat at the park?**
You can rent canoes/kayaks at Convoy Point but larger boats must be rented elsewhere.
5. **Are pets allowed in the park?**
Pets are welcome but must be leashed and not permitted on public transportation boats.
