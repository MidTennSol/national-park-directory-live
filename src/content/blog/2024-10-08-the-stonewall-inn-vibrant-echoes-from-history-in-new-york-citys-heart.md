## **Introduction**

Bearing an extraordinary weight of contemporary U.S. history, the **Stonewall National Monument** stands unassuming yet powerful at 51-53 Christopher Street in New York City's Greenwich Village. This instantly identifiable landmark hides a tumultuous story behind its brick façade; a tale that is interwoven with the city's pulse and has indelibly left its mark on American society.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

The locale’s historical significance stems from the pivotal riots that erupted outside its doors during a police raid in June 1969. These disturbances, known as the **Stonewall Riots**, marked a seminal moment for LGBTQ+ activism worldwide. The monument itself was declared by President Obama in 2016, designating it as a symbol of struggle and progress toward gay rights.

In terms of architecture, the inn is nestled within two adjoining buildings erected between 1843 and 1846. Its rustic exterior belies a rich tapestry inside - high ceilings with ornate cornices speak volumes about mid-19th century craftsmanship.

## **Activities Guide**

## **Visitor Information**

The monument comprises two primary structures: The Stonewall Inn itself and the adjacent Christopher Park. Both are open to the public 24/7, with guided tours readily available online.

While it doesn’t have dedicated parking spaces, public transport options abound due to its central location – just off Seventh Avenue South on Christopher Street.
