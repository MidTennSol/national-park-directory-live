Journey back to one of America's crucial Revolutionary War battlegrounds with a visit to Ninety Six National Historic Site. Named for the believed 96 miles distance between it and a local Cherokee settlement, this 1,022-acre national treasure transports you through time to the bloody skirmishes that decided our nation’s fate. Situated near Greenwood County, South Carolina, Ninety Six is more than just a testament to American resilience—it serves as a poignant reminder of territorial conflicts and tribulations faced by early settlers.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
The National Park Service has painstakingly preserved relics from two major Revolutionary War battles fought here—the Battle of Long Cane Creek and the Siege of Ninety Six—granting visitors an intimate look at colonial and military life. The site boasts an impressive array of surviving structures including the Star Fort, earthworks from two sieges, and remnants of two thriving towns.
Beyond its military archives, Ninety Six also speaks volumes about indigenous culture. Pre-dating European exploration, native tribes like Mississippian-era peoples used this area as their hunting grounds and left behind fascinating archaeological artifacts.

## **Activities Guide**
The rich tapestry that is Ninety Six isn’t merely a mecca for history buffs but offers something exciting for every visitor. Embark on a walking tour along 2.5-mile interpretative trail where historical markers highlight key sites such as Stockade Fort and Gouedy Trail, both brimming with stories of the past. For bird enthusiasts, a stroll along the pristine wetland trail reveals an immersive ecosystem teeming with native species.

Ninety Six National Historic Site is open year-round except for Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day.
