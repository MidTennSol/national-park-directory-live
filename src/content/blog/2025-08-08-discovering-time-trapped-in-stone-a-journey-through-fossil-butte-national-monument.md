## **Introduction**
Step back in time and unearth ancient secrets embedded in stone at the fascinating Fossil Butte National Monument. Located near Kemmerer, Wyoming — not Salt Lake City as often mistaken due to proximity — this 8,198-acre national monument holds some of Earth’s most immaculately preserved fossils. From swimming dinosaurs to early horses, these 52-million-year-old time capsules narrate a captivating tale of life's profound diversity during the Eocene epoch.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
Fossil Butte was designated a national monument in 1972 to preserve its rich fossil beds, known as 'lagerstätten'. These are rare geological formations that offer extraordinarily detailed preservation of ecological snapshots from times long past. With over 300 species of animals and plants cataloged so far from Fossil Lake—one of three great lakes during the Eocene epoch—this area provides an extraordinary snapshot into the biodiversity that once graced our planet. Unique cooling and sedimentation conditions have resulted in a remarkably well-preserved window into this bygone era, with full skeletons, soft tissues, feathers, skin—even ripple marks from ancient shores—trapped impeccably within the stony grasps of time.

## **Visitor Information**
Fossil Butte National Monument is open year-round except on major holidays. The visitor center operates from 8 am to 6 pm in summer and 9 am to 5 pm during the rest of the year. Admission is free. While there are no campgrounds within the monument boundaries, multiple options are available nearby in Kemmerer or along the Pine Creek Ski Resort road.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**
With a plethora of activities available, Fossil Butte caters to all kinds of visitors:

1. **History Buffs:** Indulge in fossil discovery activities and immerse yourself in rich geological histories at the visitors' center.
2. **Nature Enthusiasts:** Enjoy scenic drives through nature trails, observing local fauna like mule deer and pronghorns.
3. **Photographers:** Capture sunrise hues over chicken creek or sunset vistas from Cundick Ridge.
4. **Educators/Students:** Avail fascinating educational programs offered by the park rangers.

Just remember: respect the monument by leaving no trace behind.
