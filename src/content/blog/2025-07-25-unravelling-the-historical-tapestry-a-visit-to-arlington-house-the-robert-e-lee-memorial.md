## **Introduction**
Nestled amidst the verdant expanse of the historic Arlington National Cemetery is a beacon of American history – the Arlington House, The Robert E. Lee Memorial. Standing sentinel on a hill that offers sweeping views of Washington D.C., this stately mansion echoes with stories from America's past. As you enter its realm, it's like stepping onto a time machine which whisks you back to an era marked by grandeur, conflict, and transition. 

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
Begun in 1802 by George Washington Parke Custis as a living tribute to his step-grandfather, George Washington, the house was later inherited by Custis's daughter Mary Anna Randolph Custis who married acclaimed military leader Robert E. Lee. In those rooms filled with colonial charm and tranquility, the Lees raised their seven children until 1861 when Virginia seceded from the Union and initiated an unanticipated chapter in their lives.

## **Activities Guide**
While visiting Arlington House, explore each room resonating with historical significance like the parlours showcasing family artifacts or Lee's office with his military accoutrements. Step out to the meticulously kept white columns veranda for a spectacular view of Washington D.C., the Potomac River flowing like liquid silver framed by clouds and verdure.

Don't miss the North and South Slave Quarters, integral parts of the estate which shed light on the harsh realities of slavery. The guided tours offer enriching insights into the lives of slaves at Arlington House.

## **Visitor Information**

Interactive exhibits and interpretive programs are offered to provide an engaging and educational experience. Wheelchair access is available but limited due to architectural barriers in this historic property.
