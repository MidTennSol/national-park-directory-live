## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
The Port Chicago explosion was one of the most devastating home-front disasters during World War II. On July 17, 1944, an explosive mishap claimed more than 300 lives—most African-American sailors—giving birth to one of the largest mutinies in U.S. naval history, known as "Port Chicago Mutiny". The ensuing trial led to major changes in the Navy's segregation policies.

While narrating this compelling historical tale, the park also showcases an abundant variety of plant and animal life. Situated along Suisun Bay, the site boasts breathtaking views accompanied by diverse geological formations highlighting California's unique terrain.

## **Visitor Information**
The Port Chicago National Memorial is managed by the National Park Service. It requires reservations for guided tours, which typically last approximately 1.5 hours. As this memorial is located within an active military base (MOTCO), photo IDs are mandatory for individuals over 18 years old. Pets, weapons, large backpacks, and drones aren't allowed due to security restrictions.
