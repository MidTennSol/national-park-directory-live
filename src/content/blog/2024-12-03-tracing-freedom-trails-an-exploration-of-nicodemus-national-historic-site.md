## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
Nicodemus signifies more than just the settlement’s physical structures - it echoes with stories from an era when African Americans were seeking liberation after the tormented period of slavery. Founded by former slaves in 1877 during Reconstruction, Nicodemus thrived as a self-sufficient community for several decades. Yet over time, lack of infrastructure development led to its decline.

Despite these hardships, the town's spirit refused to be quenched. Its remaining residents worked tirelessly to gain recognition for their significant cultural heritage. The efforts culminated in 1976 when Nicodemus was designated the first unit of the National Park Service dedicated to African American western migration. Today, five historic buildings stand as proud sentinels telling tales from yesteryears while inspiring new generations.

Visitors are welcomed with a plethora of activities steeped in rich cultural traditions. Start your journey at _The Township Hall_, the park’s Visitor Center, where you can immerse yourself in an exhibition that traces Nicodemus's rich history.

## **Visitor Information**
Nicodemus National Historic Site is open year-round, but visiting during annual events like the _Emancipation Celebration_ or _Homecoming Weekend_ offers a unique cultural experience with music, food, storytelling and more.

The site is wheelchair-friendly with accessible parking lots and restroom facilities. Though there are no camping sites within the park, accommodations are available in nearby towns such as Hays or Bogue.

Admission to the park is free. Guided tours can be organized upon request although visitors can explore most areas independently.

   The Township Hall operates from 9am to 4:30pm, Monday through Friday. However, the park grounds are open year-round for self-guided tours.

2. **Can we picnic in the park?**
   Yes! Picnic tables are available for use near the baseball field on Washington Avenue.

3. **Are pets allowed at Nicodemus National Historic Site?**
   Yes, pets are welcome but must be kept on a leash not longer than six feet and attended at all times.

4. **Are there any items that should not be brought into the historic buildings?**
   Food, drinks (other than water), and large backpacks should not be brought into the historic buildings to help preserve them.
