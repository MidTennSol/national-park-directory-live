## **Historical/Cultural/Geographical Background**

Few places demonstrate our nation’s struggle for racial equality better than Little Rock Central High School. In 1957, amidst intense socio-political tension, nine African-American students walked these halls as part of a strategic effort to desegregate public schools. Known as the Little Rock Nine, their bravery shook the country and ignited conversations about equal educational opportunities for all races that continue today.

The school itself is an architectural marvel; an exquisite blend of Gothic and art deco styles. Today it stands not only as a functioning educational institute but also as a poignant monument to American tenacity and courage.

## **Activities Guide**

Visitors can truly absorb the spirit and significance of this historic landmark through various engaging activities. The Visitor Center offers an array of exhibits and documentaries that highlight critical events leading up to, during, and after the school’s desegregation crisis.

For those who prefer first-hand experiences, guided tours are available around the site offering vivid insights into this riveting chapter of civil rights history. Remember though — Little Rock Central High School is an active educational institution, so interior tours are limited during school sessions.

## **Visitor Information**

The site is open Monday to Saturday from 9 am to 4.30 pm, with the last film showing at 3:30 pm daily. Admission to the national historic site is free. Wheelchair-accessible facilities and sign language interpreters are available on request. It's located at 2120 Daisy L. Gatson Bates Drive in Little Rock, featuring ample parking for cars and RVs.
