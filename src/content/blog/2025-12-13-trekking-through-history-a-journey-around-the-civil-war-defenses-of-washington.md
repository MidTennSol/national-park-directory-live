## **Introduction**

Visiting these historic sites today provides not only an opportunity for outdoor activities but also offers an incredible insight into what was once one of America's most tumultuous periods - The Civil War.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
Constructed between 1861 and 1865 to protect the United States' capital during the civil war, these forts played a significant role in negotiating the course of American history. The fort circle served as scenic military defenses featuring earthen walls that often spanned hundreds of miles across hillsides with spectacular views. Today these carefully preserved landmarks serve as a vivid reminder of our nation's struggle towards unity.

Geologically speaking, each fort was ingeniously placed atop ridges or high grounds to ensure maximum visibility while remaining concealed within lush vegetation. Many of these areas have been reclaimed by forests over time but continue to bear scars from their battlefield past.

## **Activities Guide**
While steeped in rich history and culture, Civil War Defenses also offer countless outdoor activities for nature enthusiasts and explorers. Moreover, special ranger-led programs like living history demonstrations, guided walks, and torchlight tours provide a richer understanding of the site's historical significance.

## **Visitor Information**
Forts Foote, Dupont, Greble, Stanton, Ricketts, Davis, and Circle forts remain open for public visits year-round from dawn till dusk. However, access to specific areas may vary depending on restoration work or seasonal closures. 

Keep in mind that these parks don't have facilities such as restaurants or gift shops but feature restrooms at selected sites. Visitors are encouraged to wear comfortable shoes given the uneven terrain and carry water bottles during summer months.
