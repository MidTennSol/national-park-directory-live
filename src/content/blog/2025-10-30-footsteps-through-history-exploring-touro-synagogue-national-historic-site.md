## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background** 
The cornerstone of Touro Synagogue was laid in 1759, making it the oldest standing synagogue in North America. Rich with historical significance, it stands as a symbol of religious tolerance and freedom — principles enshrined in the First Amendment to the United States Constitution. One cannot overlook the letter written by George Washington to the congregation in 1790 promising that their religious liberties would be protected under this new democracy.

In 1946, recognizing its national historic value, Touro Synagogue was designated a National Historic Site. It carries not just Jewish history but interweaves stories of immigrants coming from far-off lands intersecting with mainstream American narratives.
