## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
On May 31, 1889, an unspeakable tragedy unfolded when a dam failure sent millions of tons of water rushing toward Johnstown. The resultant flood claimed over 2,200 lives and caused considerable destruction, marking it as one of the most disastrous floods in U.S. history. Today, this national memorial stands as a testament to those who lost their lives and to resilience in the face of adversity.

The beauty of this monument lies within the dichotomy between its tragic past and stunning natural backdrop. Stream-carved valleys lined with lush forests reflect Pennsylvania's geological splendor while serving as living reminders of a powerful event that has indelibly shaped local life.

## **Activities Guide**
While there is solemnity to this place due to its historical importance, interactive activities paint a vibrant picture for visitors. Start at the Visitor Center where exhibits showcase artifacts from the flood, pictures, survivor accounts and more. Participate in ranger-guided tours or take a self-guided auto tour that covers key historical sites associated with the flood.

Nature enthusiasts will enjoy exploring pristine trails teeming with native flora and fauna. Popular trails include Staple Bend Tunnel trail that leads to the nation's first railroad tunnel, or the Path of the Flood trail that traces the floodwater's journey.

## **Visitor Information**
The Memorial is open year-round except for major holidays. While there is no fee to enter, donations are welcome. Plan your visit between May and October when interpretive programs operate daily. Visitors can avail of park amenities such as picnic areas, restrooms, and bookstore.
