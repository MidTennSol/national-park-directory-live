## **Introduction**

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

Our journey begins with a trip down memory lane. Conceived as a testament to George Washington’s monumental contribution to American independence, construction began in 1848 but was not completed until 1884 due to political turmoil and lack of funds. Standing tall at over 555 feet, it was once the tallest man-made structure in the world.

Its dazzling white exterior is made from marble sourced from Maryland and Massachusetts while its interior features donation stones from every state in America along with countless international entities offering their tribute. These stones narrate tales mirroring periods of American history, each one reflecting unique cultural influences and geological distinctions.

## **Activities Guide**

Beyond its eye-catching form lives an array of activities waiting to be explored! Begin your day by ascending its elevator taking mere seconds to reach dizzying heights that reward visitors with panoramic views showcasing famous landmarks like the Lincoln Memorial, the U.S. Capitol, and the Jefferson Memorial.

## **Visitor Information**

The Washington Monument is open 365 days a year from 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM. Be prepared for potential crowds as it draws over 800,000 visitors annually. Admission to the monument is free; however, a ticket is required to ride the elevator to its apex. These can be reserved online in advance or obtained from the nearby Washington Monument Lodge.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**

For families, pack a picnic to enjoy on the lush National Mall grounds and let your little ones take part in junior ranger programs. History buffs shouldn’t miss out on exploring donation stones inside while photographers must catch golden hour hues reflecting off its marble surface.

Be mindful of mobility restrictions – older people or individuals with physical limitations may find it challenging to walk through large outdoor spaces surrounding it. Consider using wheelchairs provided by visitor faculties.

## **Regional Context**

Situated amidst several other lauded monuments and memorials in downtown D.C., The Washington Monument acts as an unmistakable compass point directing you towards exploration of nearby historical gems such as The White House, The U.S. Capitol Building, and Smithsonian Museums making your visit multi-dimensional!
