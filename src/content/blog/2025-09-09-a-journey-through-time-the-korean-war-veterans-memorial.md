## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
Inaugurated on July 27, 1995—the anniversary of the armistice that ended hostilities—it took four years for this homage to the "Forgotten War" to come alive after President George H.W. Bush broke ground in 1992. 

The Memorial is anchored by Frank Gaylord’s striking platoon of soldiers – hewn from stainless steel, carrying radios and rifles while trudging through simulated wind-swept terrain. Reflected against a black granite wall etched with ghostlike images sourced from actual war photographs; they swell to an eerie formation of thirty-eight figures—signifying the 38th parallel which Korea shares between its North and South.

Nearby stands an inscribed granite wall reminding us that "Freedom Is Not Free." It captures not only a harsh truth about human rights but also honors those who paid freedom's hefty price—more than 36,000 Americans who died during service.

Elsewhere, a circular Pool of Remembrance encourages quiet contemplation of the costs of the war—measured in lives lost and people wounded—a reminder that even the hard-fought victory had a tremendous price.

## **Activities Guide**
While visiting the Memorial, take time to view each element—the Statues, the Mural Wall, United Nations (UN) wall, and the Pool of Remembrance. Each part intertwines with others to illustrate a comprehensive narrative of courage under fire, fear, honor, and duty. 

## **Visitor Information**
The memorial is open 24 hours a day and no admission fee is required. It's especially recommended to visit during early morning or late evening hours when crowds are smaller for a more personal experience. Restrooms and vending machines are located nearby at the Lincoln Memorial.
