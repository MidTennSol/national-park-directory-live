## **Introduction**

Welcome to a voyage like no other, where time spirals backward, leading us into a land filled with untouched wilderness and geological reminiscence dating back over 40 million years. Enveloped by Eastern Oregon's rugged high desert beauty is the John Day Fossil Beds National Monument—a frozen-in-time spectacle that feels more like a painting than reality. This surreal landscape invites you to discover vivid rock formations, fossil-rich landscapes, and sleepy rivers carving their way through sprawling valleys.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

John Day Fossil Beds is not a regular park; it is a moment frozen in time. The eroding hills have unveiled layers of earth from different epochs—revealing subtropical rainforests, sap-sucking insects trapped in amber from 44 million years ago, and ancient animals that once roamed these lands. Named after the paleontologists who first unearthed its fossil treasures during the late 1860s, this monument is divided into three distinct units: Sheep Rock, Painted Hills, and Clarno.

Sheep Rock exposes us to greenish-blue palette strata hosting an abundance of fossils from prehistoric mammals—rhinos and early horses among others. Painted Hills rolls out across our field of view like an artist's canvas splashed with rust-reds, golds, blacks – each hue indicating different types of soil or environmental conditions over millennia. Finally, the towering cliffs of Clarno invite you to rewind 44 million years to when this area was home to a lush, tropical climate.

## **Activities Guide**

John Day Fossil Beds offers an array of activities for every type of traveler. For the history buffs and fossil enthusiasts, begin your journey at the Thomas Condon Paleontology Center. Here, you'll find significant exhibits showcasing 40 million years of evolution through ancient flora and fauna fossils.

Outdoor adventurers will appreciate over six hiking trails varying in length and difficulty, each offering unique perspectives on the remarkably diverse landscapes. Don't miss the Island in Time Trail in Clarno or Leaf Hill Trail in Painted Hills to observe fossils embedded in rocks.

## **Visitor Information**

The park is open year-round, though spring and fall are the most comfortable seasons to explore due to milder temperatures. Entry into the park is free. However, remember there's no campground or lodging available within the National Monument—accommodations can be found in nearby cities like Mitchell or John Day. Restrooms and picnic facilities are provided for visitors' convenience.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**

Photographers will love the chance to capture the colorful hues at sunset; painted hills glow magnificently under twilight’s soft illumination. Families should deploy a “leave no trace” philosophy to protect this delicate environment—steer clear from climbing hills or removing rocks/fossils.

For senior visitors or those with mobility issues—it's worth noting that all three units have wheelchair-accessible trails ensuring everyone can enjoy these prehistoric wonders.
