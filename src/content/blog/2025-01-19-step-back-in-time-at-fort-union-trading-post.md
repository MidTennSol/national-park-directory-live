## **Introduction**

Enveloped by the vast prairies of Williston, where Montana meets North Dakota, you'll discover an authentic tribute to America's fascinating wild west – the Fort Union Trading Post National Historic Site. This historical gem is not merely a relic; it's an evocative window into a bygone era, offering you an unforgettable journey back in time.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

Founded in 1828 by John Jacob Astor's American Fur Company, Fort Union was once the most significant fur trading post along the Upper Missouri River. For nearly four decades, it served as a rendezvous point for various tribes like Assiniboine, Mandan, Hidatsa, Ojibwa, and Cree who came to barter buffalo robes and other furs for goods like blankets, beads, guns and whiskey.

Today it stands as a meticulously reconstructed trading post amidst sweeping grasslands. The fort’s significant buildings — Bourgeois' House, Trade House and Indian Trade House — perfectly recreate the atmosphere of hustle-bustle that once occupied these posts.

## **Activities Guide**

While walking through this historic site might be enough for some visitors to absorb its essence, there are plenty more activities to immerse yourself deeper into Fort Union's vibrant past.

Join one of their enlightening ranger-guided tours or enjoy a self-guided tour using their informative brochure map. During summers, experience living history demonstrations showcasing traditional crafts like blacksmithing and beading performed by skilled interpreters donned in period attire! 

For kids, the Junior Ranger Program is an engaging opportunity to learn about mid-19th-century fur trade while earning their Junior Ranger badge. For nature lovers, enjoy bird watching or explore the flora and fauna on the park's interpretive trail.

## **Visitor Information**

Open year-round, Fort Union Trading Post welcomes history enthusiasts and curious families alike. The site’s facilities include a visitor center armed with exhibits and a bookstore brimming with educational materials.

While entry to the fort is free, special events like the annual Indian Arts Showcase and Rendezvous might require a small fee. Those planning on visiting during winter months should note that some areas may be closed due to weather conditions. Always check for updates on their official website before making your travel plans.
