## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

Manzanar's story is rooted in one of America's saddest chapters—the mass internment of citizens based solely on their descent during World War II. Following Japan’s attack on Pearl Harbor in 1941, racial fear and prejudice resulted in President Roosevelt’s Executive Order 9066, which led to the forced relocation and internment of over 120,000 Japanese Americans – two-thirds being U.S citizens.

Manzanar was one of ten 'relocation centers', housing up to 10,000 internees at a time within a one-mile-square area bounded by barbed wire fencing. The desolate and harsh landscape was transformed into an extensive self-sustaining community complete with residential blocks, farms, schools, medical facilities and more - all constructed by the internees themselves.

## **Activities Guide**

The Manzanar National Historic Site is much more than a static monument to the past. It offers a variety of engaging activities designed to deepen understanding and provoke thoughtful reflection. 

The interpretive center located in an original auditorium building hosts compelling exhibits that tell stories of those interned here. Among them, a reconstructed barrack interior that illustrates the living conditions faced by these families, an emotional experience not to be missed.
