## **Introduction**
Wake up the sleeping historian inside you as you step foot on Florida's southwestern coast, where the Braden River kisses Tampa Bay: Welcome to De Soto National Memorial. More than just an outdoor paradise, this destination is also an immersive time capsule that teleports its visitors back to 1539 when Spanish conquistador Hernando de Soto landed here.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
De Soto National Memorial is not merely a park; it's a living testament to one of America's defining moments. Named after Hernando de Soto, who led the first extensive organized expedition by Europeans into what is now the southern United States, this site marks his landing point in May 1539.

Here, the cold touch of history intertwines with nature’s warm embrace. The park's geological features are typical of Florida's coastal regions - lush mangroves, sandy beaches and sub-tropical forests teeming with diverse wildlife from ospreys to alligators. Exploring their untouched beauty feels like tracing the same steps de Soto did more than five centuries ago.

## **Activities Guide**
Don't let De Soto’s compact size fool you – there's plenty here for history buffs and nature enthusiasts alike. Guided interpretive walks let you follow in de Soto's footsteps while costumed reenactors make history come alive during winter months at Camp Uzita. Plunge into water activities like fishing from the shore or kayaking along the Manatee River.

Revisit history at De Soto's Visitor Center Museum, showcasing antiquities and recounting stories of de Soto's journeys. For hiking enthusiasts, there are several short trails such as The Nature Trail that winds along the river’s edge and offers a panoramic vista of Tampa Bay.

## **Visitor Information**
Open year-round, De Soto National Memorial welcomes you with free admission; all the more reason to add this gem to your must-visit list. The Visitor Center operates daily from 9 AM to 5 PM while guided walks and living history demonstrations occur seasonally (December through April). Pets are welcome but must be leashed.
