## **Historical/Cultural/Geographical Background**
Hampton owes its existence to one of the most powerful families of Maryland during post-Independence USA - The Ridgely family. Built in 1790 by Charles Carnan Ridgely, Hampton Mansion was once the largest private home in America. Today's historical site encompasses 43 acres of manicured gardens featuring Georgian-style architecture that beautifully contrasts with its surrounding urban landscape.

But it isn't just about luxurious living quarters; it also tells stories from behind-the-scenes – those involving generations of enslaved African Americans whose hands shaped Hampton as much as their masters did. This blend of high society living and harsh realities creates for an evocative narrative waiting to be discovered at every corner.

## **Visitor Information**
Hampton National Historic Site opens 7 days a week from sunrise to sunset, except on major holidays. Entry is free of charge with guided tours available on a first-come, first-served basis. Do check out their online calendar for any special events ongoing during your visit.

If driving, parking is readily available at no additional cost. For those opting for public transportation, the Light RailLink's Lutherville Station is just about a mile away from the park entrance.
