## **Introduction**
Nestled in the heart of Independence, Missouri is the Harry S Truman National Historic Site — an emblematic chronicle of American history. More than just a national park, it serves as a portal transporting visitors into the world of our nation’s former president. As you explore this historic site, every room, artifact, and corner whisper stories from the past, providing a unique insight into the fascinating life and times of America's 33rd President.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
The Harry S Truman National Historic Site preserves two properties significant to Truman's memorable life - his family home at 219 North Delaware Street and his grandparent’s farmstead in Grandview. The home on Delaware Street stands frozen in time, bearing witness to thirty years of presidential and personal history. It offers a unique glimpse into mid-20th century Americana and represents an era when small-town values influenced national politics.

## **Activities Guide**

Don’t miss out on participating in ranger-led programs – these interactive sessions offer in-depth insights into Truman's lifestyle, his presidency, and his post-presidential years. Keep an eye on the event calendar for annual festivities like the Truman Day celebration that commemorates his birthday.

## **Visitor Information**
The visitor center is housed at the historic firehouse where you can collect maps, brochures, and tour details. It’s recommended to begin your visit here with a short orientation film that presents an overview of Truman's life. While Delaware Street home tours require tickets, the farm site tour is self-guided and free. However, always check the latest park operating hours and ticket availability online or by calling ahead before your visit.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**
Families with kids will appreciate Junior Ranger Programs specifically designed to instill a love for history and nature among younger audiences. For visitors with mobility concerns, it’s essential to note that while the first floor of the home is accessible by wheelchair, stairs are unavoidable if you wish to see the second floor.

Photography lovers should bring their cameras along - from architectural gems to beautiful landscapes; there are plenty of picturesque spots within this national historic site.
