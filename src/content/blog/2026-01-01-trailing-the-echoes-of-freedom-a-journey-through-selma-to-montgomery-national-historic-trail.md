## **Introduction**

The Selma to Montgomery National Historic Trail isn't merely a trail—it’s a stretch of land steeped in powerful history that echoes even today. It speaks volumes about human resilience, societal change, and the constant pursuit of equality and freedom. This 54-mile trail was designated by Congress in 1996 to commemorate the events, people, and route of the 1965 Voting Rights March in Alabama. 

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

Embedded deep in the red clay soil of Alabama lies undeniable evidence of a transformative period in American history. The events that gave rise to this Heritage Trail unfolded over five pivotal days—March 7-21, 1965—the march from Selma to Montgomery became a crucial turning point in the fight for civil rights.

On Bloody Sunday (March 7), peaceful protestors attempting to cross Edmund Pettus Bridge were met with brutal force by local law enforcement officers. This event shocked the world and hastened passage of the Voting Rights Act later that year.

Journeying along this route allows you not just to learn about these historic events but also gives an intimate glimpse into Southern culture—with vibrant street art giving way to serene views of bosky cotton fields—and varied geographical terrains ranging from flat lowlands to gently rolling hillsides peppered with ancient oak trees.

## **Activities Guide**

While hiking is one way of soaking up the scenery, engaging with interpretive exhibits along five major interpretive sites provides an immersive, educational experience. Starting at Selma's Lowndes Interpretive Center, you’ll find interactive exhibits that set the historical stage for the trail. 

## **Visitor Information**

The trail is open year-round and there are no fees to explore it. However, operating hours for interpretive centers may vary, with typically shorter hours during winter months. Restrooms are available at selected locations along the trail and there are accessible facilities for persons with disabilities.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**

For history buffs: Dive deep into the rich narrative by hiring a local guide or attending Ranger-led programs available at certain sites.

If fitness is your focus: The relatively flat terrain offers a perfect long-distance walk or bike ride—don't miss out on the stunning views from Edmund Pettus Bridge!

Family travel: Bring along children to help them connect with history—interactive exhibits at the interpretive centers can make learning fun and engaging for young minds.
