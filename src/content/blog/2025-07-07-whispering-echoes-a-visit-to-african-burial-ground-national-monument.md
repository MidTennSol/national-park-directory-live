## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

Located in Lower Manhattan, mere steps from city hall, lies an archaeological marvel that once served as an eternal resting place for enslaved Africans and their descendants—from the 17th century until its closure in 1794. Known today as the African Burial Ground National Monument, this sacred site was discovered during construction excavations in 1991.

This discovery unearthed a grim chapter in American history where approximately 15,000 people were buried in what was then known as 'Negroes Burial Ground'. Painstakingly preserved against the pressures of urban development, it stands today as an icon of triumph preserving a culture's dignity and reverence for their ancestors. The monument features fascinating interactive displays filled with artifacts excavated from the site—bringing forth silenced stories echoing across time.

## **Activities Guide**

Participate in ranger-led programs tailored to provide insights into African Diaspora cultures, burial practices, and life perspectives. Delve deeper into riveting historical narratives by joining in their expertly guided tours. The monument's film screening, an arresting visual journey through its history, is a must-visit.

## **Visitor Information**

Open Tuesday to Saturday, from 10 a.m. until 4 p.m., the monument offers free admission to all those who wish to fathom America's layered narrative. Handicap access and stroller-friendly paths ensure everyone can connect with this historic site equally. Reservations aren't required for individual visitors or small groups; however, larger groups (over ten) are encouraged to call ahead.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**

Whether you're a history buff eager to uncover untold stories or a student seeking educational augmentation—the African Burial Ground National Monument caters to varied interests.

Families visiting should not miss the interactive learning modules designed specifically for children. History enthusiasts can partake in detailed study sessions arranged at the onsite research library—providing rare perspectives into African-American history.

## **Regional Context**

Situated in Manhattan—a melting pot of cultures—the monument presents as a pause amidst New York City's whirlwind charm. It provides a silent reminder of the city's historical ties with slavery and juxtaposes starkly against nearby landmarks like Wall Street, reflecting capitalism and progress.
