## **Introduction**
Welcome to San Juan Island National Historical Park - a place where beauty meets history. Set against the scenic backdrop of majestic mountains and pristine waters, it proffers a gateway into the past that is steeped in intrigue and cultural richness. Stretching across over 2,100 acres of sprawling landscapes peppered with diverse flora and fauna, this park offers an enchanting immersion into America's vibrant historical tapestry.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geographical Background**
There's more to San Juan Island than its picturesque vistas. Its geographical significance played a crucial role in shaping our country’s narrative. The archipelago lies between Canada's Vancouver Island and mainland America - a strategic location that led to the infamous Pig War standoff in 1859. 

San Juan Islands was claimed by both Britain and the United States due to ambiguous phrasing in the Oregon Treaty (1846). A dispute over an American settler shooting a pig owned by Hudson's Bay Company sparked off military deployments from both sides - thus marking the start of the Pig War. Luckily, it ended without bloodshed (save for one pig!), leading to peaceful joint occupation till 1872 when Kaiser Wilhelm I ruled in favor of America following arbitration. 

Today, remnants of English Camp and American Camp from this unique chapter are well-preserved within the park boundaries.

## **Activities Guide**
While history admirers will find ample food for thought here, there's no dearth of activities for nature lovers either. 

Step onto the rolling meadows of American Camp, where you can follow trails leading past historic military structures, lighthouses, and beaches adorned with driftwood. A myriad of wildflowers, birds, rabbits, and even whales can be spotted here.

## **Visitor Information**
The park is open throughout the year. Summer months see more visitors due to favorable weather but each season has its charm in the San Juan Islands. Visitor Centers at both sites house interesting exhibits and helpful staff who can guide you through your visit.

There is no entrance fee but do check ferry schedules in advance if traveling from either Anacortes or Friday Harbor. Remember - all artifacts and natural features are protected by law so refrain from collecting souvenirs.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**
- For history buffs: Don't miss Ranger-led walking tours available during summer months.
- For outdoor enthusiasts: Plenty of hiking trails beckon you including the South Beach trail and Mount Finlayson trail.
- For wildlife watchers: Keep your eyes on the ocean. You might catch a glimpse of resident orca whales!
- For photographers: Golden hour on Mount Young or South Beach offer photo opportunities you won't want to miss.
