## **Introduction**

Welcome to Springfield, Illinois, a city bursting with historical sites that illuminate America's past. One such treasure is the Lincoln Home National Historic Site that beckons history enthusiasts, students, and curious tourists from around the globe. This four-block historic district was home to none other than the 16th President of the United States — Abraham Lincoln. Here lies not just a house but a story etched in every brick and beam.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geographical background**

In 1844, a young lawyer named Abraham Lincoln purchased this Greek revival dwelling for $1,500. Over seventeen years — critical years shaping his political philosophy — Lincoln lived here with his wife Mary Todd and their four sons. 

The house paints an intimate portrait of their family life while examining pressing issues such as slavery which profoundly influenced young Abraham's standpoint. Nestled amidst other period homes on an authentic-looking street, it effortlessly transports visitors back to mid-19th-century America.

## **Activities Guide**

Upon entering this hallowed site, one embarks on guided tours led by knowledgeable park guides narrating tales of Old Abe’s life within these walls. The various rooms preserved with personal artifacts offer a poignant glimpse into both their daily routines and significant moments.

Explore outdoor exhibits like Dean House and Arnold House interpretive centers or meander down the wooden sidewalks for self-guided neighborhood tours. A short film at Visitor Center affords a broader understanding of Lincoln's path from a Springfield lawyer to the President.

## **Visitor Information**

Open daily from 8:30 am to 5 pm, except for selected holidays, the site invites visitors year-round. Admission to house tours is free, although tickets are required due to limited capacities. It's advised to procure them early in the day at the Visitor Center as they rapidly sell out.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**

1. History aficionados should plan a full day, soaking up knowledge and strolling leisurely amidst historical surroundings.
2. Families with children can partake in the Junior Ranger Program, making learning an engaging activity.
3. Wheelchair users may need assistance due to old-fashioned doorsills but rest assured this site is committed to accessibility.
