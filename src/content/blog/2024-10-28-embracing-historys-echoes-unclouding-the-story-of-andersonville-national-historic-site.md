## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

Today's site consists of three main components: the historic Prison Site and surroundings; the National Cemetery honoring both known and unknown victims; and The National Prisoner of War Museum - the only museum dedicated to interpreting the American Prisoner Of War experience throughout history. 

Over time nature has worked her miracles to soften this landscape's harsh realities from yesteryears. Towering pines whisper tales told by winds while rugged soil underfoot cradles tales yet untold.

## **Activities Guide**
Enrich your visit with self-guided tours around Camp Sumter's remains and the Andersonville National Cemetery. A stroll through these grounds adds quietude to your historical exploration, punctuated with markers narrating poignant stories.

The National POW museum enlightens visitors about heroism, survival, and the relentless human spirit amid captivity—experience impactful exhibits like the "Hall of Valor" or the theatre showing the narrative movie - 'Echoes of Captivity'.

Budding historians can engage in Junior Ranger programs that educate young minds about this site's history, raising conscientious future citizens.

## **Visitor Information**

Entry is free to all three components. Guided tours are self-paced since there is no official park guide service. Accommodations are available in nearby Americus - which prides itself on Southern charm and hospitality.
