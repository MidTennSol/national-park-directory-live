## **Introduction**
Welcome to a journey into a pivotal chapter in American history, where segregation was outlawed and educational equality took center stage – welcome to Brown v. Board of Education National Historical Park. Situated in Topeka, Kansas, this memorial stands as a beacon and remembrance of one of the most significant Supreme Court decisions made in American history.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
For those who are unfamiliar with it, it all began on May 17, 1954 when the US Supreme Court declared "separate but equal" education unconstitutional under **Brown v. Board of Education**, overthrowing racial segregation in public schools across the nation. This ruling marked a turning point in the fight for civil rights and precipitated changes still felt today.

The park itself is housed within Monroe Elementary School which was one among four segregated schools for African Americans then existing in Topeka. Today, it serves as a potent reminder that freedom and equality are fought-for rights rather than given privileges, offering visitors an opportunity to pay tribute to this seminal moment in American Civil Rights history through its exhibits and tours.

## **Activities Guide**
Venture out into this historical landscape that has been carefully molded into a multi-dimensional experience for visitors. As you walk along halls adorned with pictures and narratives depicting pre-1954 racial segregation, be prepared for your emotions to be stirred and your perspective broadened. 

Interactive galleries, exhibits with original artefacts, oral histories that let you hear from people who were involved in the case and a film narrating the history of segregation in America awaits you here. The 'Hall of Courage' is a particularly striking part of the park recreating an intimidating walkway into a segregated high school. An array of educational programs, ranger-led discussions and interactive displays make this location far more than just a static remembrance of the past.
