## **Introduction**

Escape into a realm where tales of yesteryears and breathtaking landscapes weave an enchanting tale. Welcome to the Keweenaw National Historical Park (KNHP), a sanctuary nestled in Michigan's upper peninsula, offering an enriching blend of historical insights and awe-inspiring vistas.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

The KNHP is more than just another national park; it is a testament to America's industrial age and the indomitable human spirit. Recognized for its copper mining heritage dating back nearly 7,000 years ago to Indigenous miners, it entered the modern era with the profitable mining operations initiated by Washington investors in 1843. The park encompasses numerous sites—mines, mills, towns, rural homesteads—that narrate riveting tales of cultural exchange, entrepreneurship, technological innovation, and labor struggles that shaped America's industrial landscape.

This region's geology is equally captivating. As part of one of the world’s largest lava flows—known as the Mid-Continent Rift—the area offers awe-inspiring views of ancient volcanic formations interspersed with serene forest landscapes.

## **Activities Guide**

Visitors to KNHP have an array of activities to choose from. History enthusiasts can stroll down memory lane visiting the Quincy Mine or delve into past times at one of the local heritage museums such as Coppertown USA Mining Museum or Houghton County Historical Museum. 

Nature lovers can embark on an unforgettable journey, exploring the breathtaking vistas of the park's rich forest landscape. From easy paths to more challenging terrains, there is a trail to suit every hiker. Foremost among these are Adventure Mine and Delaware Copper Mine tours that offer both surface and underground explorations.

## **Visitor Information**
