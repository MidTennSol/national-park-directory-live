## **Introduction**

Nestled in Watrous, a serene town just over an hour northeast of Santa Fe, New Mexico lies an often overlooked gem - the Fort Union National Monument. This sprawling ruin echoes with tales from the bygone era of the American West. Here, history feels alive; it whispers in the wind that rustles prairie grass and resonates with every step you take on the ancient Santa Fe Trail. 

## **Historical/Cultural/Geographical Background**

Established in 1851 on the cusp of the Mora and Sapello rivers at their confluence, Fort Union was strategically situated to protect commerce along the path that connected Missouri to Santa Fe - known today as The Santa Fe Trail. As a hub for wagon trains carrying goods and settlers seeking fortunes out west, the fort held immense significance. 

The adobe fort we see today is actually third in line - two previous forts bearing its namesake were constructed but eventually deemed unsuitable. The existing ruins are remnants from 1863 when it was rebuilt as a larger military garrison during turbulent times marked by Civil War and native tribe conflicts.

## **Activities Guide**

Steeped in history though it may be, there's more to Fort Union than meets the eye. Start your visit at the Visitor Center where exhibits uncover historical anecdotes about life at this frontier outpost. A short film paints a vivid picture of its important role. Armed with this knowledge, head out towards self-guided trails meandering through ruins whispering tales of soldiers, settlers, and supply wagons. 

The monument also plays host to an array of annual events such as the Candlelight Tours that provide unique insights into soldier life at night in the 19th century and Fort Union Days showcasing historical reenactments.

## **Visitor Information**

The park is open year-round from dawn till dusk, with the Visitor Center operating from 8 am to 4 pm. Entry is free of cost which is a rare treat among National Parks. But remember, there are no food services available within the park so pack accordingly. Do not forget your sunblock and hats; New Mexico’s sun can be unrelenting!
