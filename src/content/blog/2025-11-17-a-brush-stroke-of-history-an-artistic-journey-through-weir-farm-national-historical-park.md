## **Introduction**
Immerse yourself in the simplistic charm and creative legacy of Weir Farm National Historical Park tucked away in idyllic Wilton, Connecticut. Spanning across 60 acres, this picturesque landscape is an homage to creativity, liberty, and the pursuit of beauty. It stands testament to three generations of American painters: Julian Alden Weir, Mahonri Young, and Sperry Andrews – making it the only national park dedicated solely to American painting.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
Weir Farm was purchased by Julian Alden Weir, one of America's pioneering Impressionists, in 1882. It served as his rural retreat and source of inspiration for many renowned masterpieces. Later on, his daughter Dorothy Weir Young and her husband Mahonri Young, continued using the farm as their artistic haven. In 1957, the farm transferred hands again when New England painters Sperry and Doris Andrews became its stewards.

The park possesses a rich tapestry weaved with cultural histories and geological wonders. Glacial erratics dotting the landscape remind us that this area was once underneath a massive ice sheet during the last Ice Age.

## **Activities Guide**

Visit during summer months to witness historians in period dress recreating a day in the life of Julian Weir. From bird-watching, nature photography to hiking along well-marked trails, there's something for everyone!

## **Visitor Information**
Weir Farm National Historical Park is open year-round, with park grounds accessible from sunrise to sunset. The visitor center is open Wednesday through Sunday, from 10:00 am to 4:00 pm. Guided tours for the Weir house and studio come at no cost but require advanced reservations due to the ongoing pandemic.
