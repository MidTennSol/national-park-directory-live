## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
Nestled between America's coastlines lies a story that dates back to over four centuries ago. Before Jamestown and Plymouth existed, there was the Saint Croix Island- a destination chosen for settlement by Samuel de Champlain and Pierre Dugua Sieur de Mons. Their choice wasn't random; the island offered rich soil, ample fish stocks, and was reasonably distant from hostile native tribes.

However, what seemed like a haven initially turned out to be quite challenging due to severe winter conditions leading to scurvy and death among settlers. The survivors moved in 1605 onward but left behind a rich lineage that shaped America's future colonies.

The island's natural beauty is mesmerizing. From its lush green forests to sparkling river water surrounding it - here Mother Nature showcases her creativity at its best!

## **Visitor Information**
Saint Croix Island International Historic Site is open all around the year. There's no entrance fee and you can always take advantage of one of the guided tours organized by park rangers.

Despite its remote location, visitors can access modern facilities such as restrooms, picnic areas, and an interpretive trail equipped with signages in French and English along the way. Please note, actual access to the island is prohibited to preserve its fragile ecosystem.
