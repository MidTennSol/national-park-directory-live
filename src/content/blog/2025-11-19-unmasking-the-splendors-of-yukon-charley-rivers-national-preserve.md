## **Introduction**

Tucked away in eastern Alaska's Fairbanks region lies an unspoiled jewel that beckons to both thrill-seekers and serene solitude seekers alike – the Yukon - Charley Rivers National Preserve. This haven of untouched wilderness is more than just a park; it's a testament to nature's awe-inspiring grandeur and resilience.

Spanning 2.5 million acres, the preserve is defined by two major water bodies from which it draws its name – the mighty Yukon River and its tributary, the Charley River. With a landscape dotted with towering cliffs, rugged hills, verdant forests, and sparkling rivers, this preserve is nature’s canvas painted with breathtaking strokes of wild beauty.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

Yukon-Charley Rivers National Preserve holds invaluable historical significance as a time capsule that narrates stories from different eras. It once cradled nomadic tribes such as Han Athabascans who left behind remnants of their culture through archaeological sites atop high bluffs overlooking Yukon River.

The advent of gold rush days brought miners who constructed cabins and steamboats along the riverbanks, some of which still stand today as nostalgic memorials to a bygone era. 

## **Activities Guide**

Whether you desire adrenaline-pumping adventure or peaceful solitude, the Yukon-Charley offers boundless activities. Glide along the Yukon or Charley River in a kayak or raft, taking in the scenic vistas. 

Hikers can enjoy the different terrains, from challenging mountain trails to gentle woodland paths. Wildlife enthusiasts will relish tracing bear, moose, and lynx footprints to secluded spots where they can observe these majestic creatures in their natural habitat.

Winter transforms the preserve into a snow-covered wonderland ideal for activities like dog sledding and snowmobile riding while offering pristine locations for those interested in ice fishing or winter photography.

## **Visitor Information**

Owing to its remote location, planning your trip is crucial. Fairbanks serves as the most accessible gateway with Eagle and Circle being the nearest small towns. Once arrived, get oriented at any of three visitor contact stations: Coal Creek Camp (summer only), Slaven's Roadhouse (winter-only), and Eagle Visitor Center (year-round).

Unless you're an experienced wilderness traveler who knows how to manage risks associated with river travel or bears, guided tours are advisable. Remember, this park has no established trails or signs outside of Coal Creek Camp mines.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**

Families with children should focus on safer recreational activities such as wildlife viewing from boats and easy hikes near campgrounds. Senior visitors might enjoy cultural learning experiences through historical spot visits or panning for gold like early miners did.

Photographers should prepare themselves for changing weather conditions since both sunrise and sunset could offer fantastic shooting opportunities. Lastly, adventure seekers must remember that safety comes first; always respect nature's might while enjoying her beauty.

## **Regional Context**

Situated between two national parks—Gates of Arctic National Park to the north and Wrangell-St.Elias National Park & Preserve to the south—Yukon-Charley offers a distinctively untouched and uncommercialized wilderness experience. Its astounding biodiversity, preserved historical remnants, and splendid isolation make it an indispensable part of your Alaskan adventure.
