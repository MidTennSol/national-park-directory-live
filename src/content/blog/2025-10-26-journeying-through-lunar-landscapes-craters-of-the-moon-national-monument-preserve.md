## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

Established by President Calvin Coolidge in 1924, the park safeguards a vast array of lava fields, spanning across almost 750,000 acres. These were created by volcanic eruptions that started about 15,000 years ago and ended mere 2000 years back – practically yesterday from a geological perspective! 

Native American tribes frequented this region for hunting before it was engulfed by lava. But even post-eruptions, they incorporated this transformed land into their myths and legends, leading to fascinating stories that still echo along the park's trails.

## **Activities Guide**

The Craters offer myriad activities for every kind of traveler. Those fascinated by different geological formations can take up trails such as 'North Crater Flow Trail' or 'Inferno Cone Lookout.' In winter months these trails transform into snowshoeing paths. 

The park's 'Wilderness Area' and the 'Indian Tunnel,' a large lava tube, are popular among hikers and photographers for their surreal spaces. Night sky viewing events, wildlife spotting, and guided walks across the landscapes are other unique adventures that one can embark upon here.

## **Visitor Information**

Open year-round, the park offers various amenities – from a visitor center and museum to picnic areas and campgrounds. A small entrance fee is charged, although free admission days are available on certain notable dates.

It bears mentioning that due to its unique landscape, potential hazards do exist in the form of sharp rocks and unstable surfaces. Therefore, visitors are urged to wear sturdy footwear and always be mindful of safety guidelines provided by the park staff.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**

First-time visitors should certainly take advantage of ranger-led programs providing insights into the park's geology. Photographers aiming for best shots must visit during sunrise or sunset when an ethereal glow transforms this moonscape further. Families with young kids will find it worthwhile to participate in Junior Ranger programs offering educational activities tailored for children.

## **Regional Context**

Located approximately 84 miles west of Idaho Falls, Craters Of The Moon National Monument & Preserve serves as an ideal stopover during your road trip through southern Idaho.
