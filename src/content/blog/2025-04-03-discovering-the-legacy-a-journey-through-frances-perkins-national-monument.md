## **Introduction**
Capturing the essence of early twentieth-century America and drawing from the abundant wellspring of human spirit, Frances Perkins National Monument stands proudly on the outskirts of Augusta, Maine. This elegantly understated site is named in honor of Frances Perkins, who served as Secretary of Labor under President Franklin D. Roosevelt and played an instrumental role in creating social safety nets for American citizens. The monument invites visitors into an intimate connection with this notable woman's life and work.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geographical Background**
Nestled amidst serene woods, the property was owned by Perkins from 1926 until her death in 1965. Symbolizing persistence against adversity, strength amid hardship, and steadfast resolve to uplift society, this national monument embodies a period that transformed American lives forever—the New Deal era.

The grounds present a physical manifestation of timeless Maine beauty, showcasing lush landscapes dotted with native flora and fauna. Meanwhile, preserved structures like a red-brick chimney and stone walls whisper tales of yore against the backdrop of awe-inspiring boulders tamed by glacial movements centuries ago.

## **Activities Guide**
The monument offers history buffs an unrivaled walk through time while nature enthusiasts can relish exploratory hikes across its woodland trails. Guided tours available at the site provide ample insight into Perkins' life and political feats. Around the homestead's remains are interpretive signs unfurling stories about her roles in drafting landmark policies like Social Security Act or Fair Labor Standards Act.

Birdwatchers will appreciate the plethora of avian species; ardent photographers can capture snapshots of timeless beauty as seasons wheel through their vibrant transitions. And everyone is sure to revel in a quiet picnic on the tranquil grounds.

## **Visitor Information**
The Frances Perkins National Monument is open all year round, and admission is free. Accessibility for wheelchair users or those with mobility impairments is provided wherever possible. Restrooms and basic services are available on site, but visitors must bring their own food and water.
