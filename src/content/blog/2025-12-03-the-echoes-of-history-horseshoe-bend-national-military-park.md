## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

The significance of the park stems from an intense battle that occurred here in 1814. As part of the War of 1812, over 800 Upper Creek or Red Stick Creek Indian warriors perished while defending their homeland against General Andrew Jackson's army. Known as The Battle of Horseshoe Bend, it marked both the culmination of the Creek War and served as a stepping stone towards moving Native Americans towards western territories.

## **Visitor Information**

The Park is operational all year, except for Christmas and New Year's Day. Entrance fees apply per vehicle and are valid for seven days. Affordable annual passes are also available if you're planning repeat visits during your stay in Alabama. Restrooms and drinking water facilities are located at both the entrance and exit of the park.
