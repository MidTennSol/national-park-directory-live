## **Introduction**
It is not every day one gets to travel back in time to an era when colossal creatures roamed the Earth, ruling over a world untamed and unexplored. Yet, that is precisely what awaits you at the alluring expanse of **Dinosaur National Monument**. Spanning across two states; Colorado and Utah, this unique space encapsulates nature's timeless beauty alongside tangible reminders of prehistoric life.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
The story of Dinosaur National Monument is written in stone – quite literally! Discovered in 1909 by Earl Douglass, a paleontologist from the Carnegie Museum, this rich fossil deposit showcases sedimentary rocks dating back to the Jurassic era. It was proclaimed a national monument by President Woodrow Wilson in 1915 to preserve its invaluable collection of dinosaur bones. Today, over 1,500 fossils embedded in a cliff wall are on display at the Quarry Exhibit Hall.

Flanking these treasures are layers upon layers of red sandstone intersected with stripes of grey-blue shale – an echo from drastically different climatic periods millions of years ago. All around you, canyons carved by rivers stretching miles add depth to this stirring geological tale.

## **Activities Guide**
Whether you're an avid adventurer or prefer tranquil escapades amidst natural splendor, there is something for everyone. Hiking trails like Harper’s Corner trail offer both fascinating geology and stunning vistas over Green River. For more leisurely pursuits, camping under the star-studded sky of Echo Park, surrounded by towering sandstone cliffs is a must. 

If water sports get your adrenaline pumping, try white-water rafting on Yampa or Green Rivers. For heritage lovers, petroglyphs and pictographs along Cub Creek are sure to intrigue. And don't forget the centerpiece - Quarry Exhibit Hall, a temporary home for fossils awaiting excavation.

## **Visitor Information**
The Monument is open year-round, with differing activities depending on the season. While spring and fall favor hiking and rafting, winters bring about cross-country skiing and snowshoeing opportunities.

Camping facilities at Rainbow Park, Deerlodge Park, and Green River support both tent camping and RVs. Visitor centers at Quarry and Canyon offer useful information.

There’s an entry fee per vehicle for seven days which can vary seasonally. The Quarry Exhibit Hall requires separate tickets that include guided tours.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**
For fossil fanatics: Arriving early in the morning will get you ahead of crowds at Quarry Exhibit Hall.

Nature photographers: Sunset at Harper's Corner offers breathtaking views as the setting sun casts a warm glow on surrounding canyons – an absolute delight for your lens!

Families with kids: Junior Ranger programs are excellent for involving young ones in fun learning activities

Backpackers: Make sure to check-in beforehand with park rangers before embarking on lengthy hikes or backcountry camping trips.
