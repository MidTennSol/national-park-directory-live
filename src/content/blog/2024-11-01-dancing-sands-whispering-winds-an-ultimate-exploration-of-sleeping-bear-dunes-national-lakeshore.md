## **Introduction**
Nestled on Lake Michigan's northeastern coastline lies an ethereal landscape brimming with natural beauty and intriguing folklore - the Sleeping Bear Dunes National Lakeshore. This biodiverse haven stretches over 35 miles of pristine shoreline and encompasses towering sand dunes that give way to lush forests, thriving wetlands, and crystalline waters that mirror a sky full of endless possibilities.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
The national lakeshore derives its poetic name from Ojibwe legend - a tale of a mother bear (Sleeping Bear) and her two cubs escaping Wisconsin fires by swimming across Lake Michigan. The mother reached the shores but her cubs were lost to fatigue, forming what we now know as North and South Manitou Islands.

Over millennia, relentless lake currents sculpted the landscape into majestic perched dunes while glaciers gifted the area an impressive array of plant diversity. In 1970, this unique convergence of geology and story was federally protected under the law, safeguarding it as one of America’s most cherished national treasures.

## **Activities Guide***
Embrace your inner adventurer at Sleeping Bear Dunes! Walk along iconic Pierce Stocking Scenic Drive or challenge yourself climbing up Dune Climb – known for its panoramic views atop sun-kissed sands. Traverse the striking Manitou Islands, explore the Colonial-era lighthouses, and unearth human history at historic farmsteads and maritime museums.

Crystal clear waters invite kayaking and paddle boarding while winter offers snowshoeing and cross-country skiing opportunities.

## **Visitor Information**
Sleeping Bear Dunes is open year-round with a $25 vehicle permit valid for seven days. With two campgrounds and numerous hotels in nearby towns, accommodation options abound. Don’t forget to stop by Philip A Hart Visitor Center for detailed park info, exhibits, bookstores and ranger-led activities.
