## **Introduction**

Tucked away amidst Savannah's breathtaking coastal landscape lies an extraordinary piece of American history - the Fort Pulaski National Monument. This massive brick stronghold has borne witness to some of the most dramatic events in our nation’s past. Once a pivotal battleground during the Civil War, today it offers visitors a unique blend of educational immersion and outdoor fun.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**

Constructed between 1829 and 1847 on Cockspur Island, Fort Pulaski was named after Kazimierz Pulaski, a Polish hero who fought for America's independence. Despite its impressive construction — with walls seven-and-a-half feet thick made of solid brick — its military invulnerability was shattered during the Civil War when Union forces introduced new rifled cannon technology. 

The battle marked a turning point in military history as it rendered masonry forts obsolete overnight. After this siege in 1862, the fort was used as a prison for captured Confederate soldiers before being abandoned post-war until its revival as a national monument in 1924.

The surrounding geography is just as stunning as its history is intriguing; located between the South Channel of the Savannah River and Tybee Island's seaside marshlands, the monument sits amidst lush greenery and serene waterways teeming with local wildlife.

## **Activities Guide**

While history buffs revel in reenactments and guided tours within the fort, nature enthusiasts can explore the salt marshes, tidal creeks, and wooded trails that densely populate the surrounding 5,600 acres. For bird watchers, it's a paradise with over 200 species of birds documented here.

Hiking along the McQueen's Island Historic Trail offers breathtaking coastal views. You can even spot bottlenose dolphins frolicking nearby during your trek. For those who prefer aquatic escapades, canoeing and kayaking provide an invigorating experience through quiet waterways.

## **Visitor Information**

The Fort Pulaski National Monument is open year-round except on Thanksgiving Day, Christmas Day, and New Year’s Day. The standard entrance fee is $10 for adults aged 16 or older but admission is free for children aged 15 and under. 

Guided tours are available throughout the day beginning at the Visitors Center where you can also browse through an array of artifacts and displays or enjoy a short film about the monument’s history.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**

If you're visiting with kids, make sure to participate in the Junior Ranger program where they can learn about ecology and Civil War history while earning a badge. Photography enthusiasts should bring their gear as sunrise or sunset paints an arresting picture against the fort walls.

Bring appropriate outdoor equipment like binoculars for birdwatching and sturdy footwear for trekking as well as sunscreen during summer months.

History lovers would enjoy participating in a ranger-led guided tour that provides intimate details of significant events that have taken place at this venerable monument.

## **Regional Context**

Savannah is one of Georgia's oldest cities known for its rich cultural heritage and southern charm. The Fort Pulaski National Monument forms an integral part of this historic fabric. Within its proximity are attractions like Tybee Island's sandy beaches or Savannah's downtown marked by Antebellum architecture and Spanish moss-draped public squares.

Just across Savannah River lies South Carolina, offering coastal attractions like Hilton Head Island making Fort Pulaski an excellent stopover during a regional trip.

## **Conclusion**

The Fort Pulaski National Monument offers more than just insight into America’s Civil War history; it provides visitors with a vast expanse of outdoor excitement, natural beauty, and serenity.
