## **Introduction**
Unveiled on Armistice Day, November 11th, 2021, the imposing World War I Memorial stands majestically on Pennsylvania Avenue in Washington D.C. The memorial honors the 116,516 Americans who paid an extreme sacrifice during something historians often describe as "the war to end all wars." 

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
The WWI Memorial is a testament to strength and endurance. Its historical background is shrouded in tales of courage, with each attribute acting as tribute to those who fought valiantly on foreign soils from 1917-1918 hoping for a safer world. Architecturally influenced by classical styles yet imbued with modern sensibilities, the central feature is a beautiful bronze sculpture named “A Soldier’s Journey”, created by eminent sculptor Sabin Howard.

Carved from granite and steel, it showcases the geological resilience matching that of our brave soldiers. Encompassing elements from both French and American landscapes - where much of the battles took place - it is rich not just historically but geographically too.

## **Visitor Information**
The WWI Memorial is open to visitors year-round. Admission is free with no tickets required for entry. The closest Metro stations are Federal Triangle and Smithsonian, both an easy walk to the memorial. Parking around the monument can be limited; public transport or taxis are a more reliable option. Restrooms, food services, and first aid facilities are available on-site.

## **Tips for Different Visitors**
For families with children: Take advantage of the Junior Ranger Program offered by National Park Service providing an engaging learning experience centering around history of WWI.

For seniors or persons with mobility issues: Wheelchair-accessible routes access all parts of the memorial.

For photographers: Mornings provide best light casting a beautiful glow on bronze wall – a perfect opportunity for memorable pictures.
