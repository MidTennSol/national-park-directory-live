## **Introduction**
Welcome to the heart of America's Civil Rights Heritage. Nestled within the cityscape of Anniston, Alabama, stands a monument steeped in historical significance—the Freedom Riders National Monument. Opened to public access in 2017, this national treasure extends beyond being just a tangible reminder of our history; it serves as a testament to bravery, equality, and the enduring spirit of those who fought for civil rights.

## **Historical/Cultural/Geological Background**
On May 14th, 1961, the color and course of American history were forever altered when resistance met grit and determination – all happening on what would later be recognized as the hallowed grounds of the Freedom Riders National Monument. 

The monument encompasses two separate sites – the Greyhound Bus Station where a racially integrated busload of Freedom Riders aiming to challenge racial segregation was violently attacked—and the location six miles away where their bus was set ablaze by protesters opposing integration.

Today these sites—once scenes of terror and defiance—stand silently echoing stories of courage and all that it took for Americans to win their hard-earned freedoms.

Remember, this isn't merely a sightseeing trip – it's an invitation to embark on a pilgrimage of understanding, reflection, and respect for a relentless struggle for equality.

## **Visitor Information**
When planning your visit to the Freedom Riders National Monument, consider that both locations are open-air sites without visitor centers. The Greyhound Bus Station area features historical signage, while the bus burning site is still primarily undeveloped except for the commemorative marker.

While admission is free with no specific operating hours or seasons, remember to respect these places' sanctity as you wander and discover.
